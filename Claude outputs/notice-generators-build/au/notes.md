## Act: what was read

- APP 1 (Schedule 1): practices, procedures and systems for compliance and for inquiries and complaints (1.2);
  a clearly expressed and up-to-date APP privacy policy (1.3); it must contain (1.4): (a) the kinds of personal
  information collected and held; (b) how it is collected and held; (c) the purposes for which it is
  collected, held, used and disclosed; (d) how to access and seek correction; (e) how to complain about a
  breach of the APPs or a binding registered APP code, and how the entity will deal with the complaint;
  (f) whether it is likely to disclose personal information to overseas recipients; (g) if so, the countries,
  if practicable to specify them. Free of charge and in an appropriate form (1.5; the note: usually on the
  website); a copy in a requested form (1.6).
- APP 2: the option of anonymity or a pseudonym, unless a law requires identification or it is impracticable.
- APP 3.3: sensitive information only with consent and where reasonably necessary (organisations), or an
  exception in 3.4. Sensitive information (s. 6(1)): racial or ethnic origin, political opinions or
  membership, religious or philosophical beliefs, professional or trade association or trade union
  membership, sexual orientation or practices, criminal record, health, genetic information, biometric
  information for automated verification or identification, biometric templates.
- APP 5: at or before collection, or as soon as practicable after, reasonable steps to notify or make aware of
  the 5.2 matters that are reasonable: (a) identity and contact details; (b) collection from someone else, or
  where the person may not be aware, the fact and circumstances; (c) collection required or authorised by an
  Australian law or court/tribunal order, with its name or details; (d) purposes; (e) the main consequences if
  not collected; (f) the entities, bodies or persons, or types, to which it usually discloses such
  information; (g) that the privacy policy explains access and correction; (h) that the privacy policy
  explains complaints and how they are dealt with; (i) whether it is likely to disclose to overseas
  recipients; (j) if so, the countries if practicable.
- APP 6.1: use or disclosure for a secondary purpose only with consent or an exception (6.2 related or
  directly related and reasonably expected, law, permitted general or health situations, enforcement).
- APP 7 (organisations): no direct marketing unless 7.2 (collected from the person, reasonably expected, a
  simple means to opt out, no request made), 7.3 (otherwise: consent or impracticable, an opt-out and a
  prominent statement in each communication), 7.4 (sensitive information: consent), 7.5 (Commonwealth
  contracts). 7.6: people can ask not to receive direct marketing, not to have their information used or
  disclosed to facilitate others' direct marketing, and the source of the information; 7.7 free, within a
  reasonable period. 7.8: displaced to the extent the Spam Act 2003, the Do Not Call Register Act 2006 and
  the Interactive Gambling Act apply.
- APP 8: before disclosing to an overseas recipient (not the entity itself or the individual), reasonable
  steps so that the recipient does not breach the APPs (8.1); s. 16C makes the entity accountable. Not
  required where (8.2): (a) the entity reasonably believes the recipient is subject to a law or binding
  scheme substantially similar overall, with mechanisms the individual can access to enforce it; (aa) 8.3
  (a country or binding scheme prescribed by the regulations, with any conditions); (b) the entity expressly
  informs the individual that if they consent 8.1 will not apply, and they consent; (c) required or
  authorised by Australian law or a court/tribunal order; (d) a permitted general situation (not items 4, 5);
  (e), (f) agencies only.
- APP 11.2: destroy or de-identify personal information no longer needed for any permitted purpose, unless a
  Commonwealth record or a law requires retention; 11.3: steps include technical and organisational measures.
- APP 12: access on request (12.1); response: agencies within 30 days, organisations within a reasonable
  period; in the manner requested if reasonable and practicable (12.4); other means where refused (12.5,
  12.6); agencies cannot charge (12.7); organisations: a charge for giving access must not be excessive and
  not for making the request (12.8); written reasons and the complaint mechanisms on refusal (12.9).
- APP 13: correction (13.1); notifying other APP entities on request (13.2); written reasons and complaint
  mechanisms on refusal (13.3); a statement associated with the information on request (13.4); response
  within 30 days (agencies) or a reasonable period (organisations); no charge (13.5).
- s. 6D: a small business has annual turnover of $3,000,000 or less (6D(1), (2)); a small business operator
  is not an organisation bound by the APPs (s. 6C) unless 6D(4) applies: a business over $3,000,000 in an
  earlier year, a health service holding health information, trading in personal information for a benefit,
  a Commonwealth contracted service provider, a credit reporting body (subject to 6D(5) to (8)).
- s. 5B(1A) to (3): the Act extends to acts and practices outside Australia of an organisation with an
  Australian link: an Australian citizen or permanent resident, a partnership, trust or body corporate formed
  in Australia, an association managed there, or an organisation that carries on business in Australia.

## Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (No. 128, 2024), as made

Read on legislation.gov.au (EPUB HTML fetched in the page). Assent 10 December 2024. Schedule 1, Parts 1 to 7
from 11 December 2024 (objects, APP codes, emergency declarations, children's privacy, security, retention
and destruction (APP 11.3), overseas data flows (APP 8.2(aa), 8.3), eligible data breaches). Schedule 1,
Part 15 (automated decisions and privacy policies) from 10 December 2026: new APP 1.7 to 1.9 (items 87 to 89):

- 1.7: the APP privacy policy must contain the 1.8 information if (a) the entity has arranged for a computer
  program to make, or do a thing substantially and directly related to making, a decision; (b) the decision
  could reasonably be expected to significantly affect the rights or interests of an individual; and (c)
  personal information about the individual is used in the operation of the program for it.
- 1.8: (a) the kinds of personal information used in the operation of such programs; (b) the kinds of such
  decisions made solely by the operation of such programs; (c) the kinds of such decisions for which a thing
  substantially and directly related to making the decision is done by such programs.
- 1.9: deciding includes refusing or failing to decide; effects can be adverse or beneficial; examples: a
  decision under an Act or instrument to grant or refuse a benefit; a decision affecting rights under a
  contract, agreement or arrangement; a decision affecting access to a significant service or support.
- Item 89: applies to decisions made after 10 December 2026, whenever the program was arranged or the data
  acquired. Item 87: APP 1.7 becomes a civil penalty provision through s. 13K(1)(b)(iia) (infringement
  notices).
- Schedule 2 (statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy) commenced by proclamation or after 6 months;
  not a notice matter. Schedule 3: doxxing offences in the Criminal Code.

## APP Guidelines, Chapter 1, version 2.0 (updated 30 September 2026), read in the browser

Read on oaic.gov.au, paras 1.8 to 1.63 (the old v1.1 notes above stay for history; v2.0 governs).

- 1.23: the access and correction contact should at least give a position title, telephone number, postal
  address and email address; a generic contact (not a named person) is fine.
- 1.27: a binding registered APP code must be named in the policy.
- 1.28: complaints: first in writing to the entity (s 40(1A)), usually 30 days to respond, then a recognised
  external dispute resolution scheme the entity belongs to, then the OAIC.
- 1.30 to 1.33: "likely" overseas disclosure means current practice or established plans; a disclosure to a
  related body corporate overseas counts; routing through an overseas server or the entity's own overseas
  office is a use, not a disclosure; countries listed if practicable, else an appendix, a link or general
  regions, and impracticability has to be justified.
- 1.35 to 1.39: APP 1.7 to 1.9 apply from 10 December 2026 to decisions made after that date; all three
  conditions in 1.7 must be met; the 1.8 items are kinds of information and kinds of decisions, in general
  terms, not the logic of the program.
- 1.40: a human who uses a program for other purposes (a word processor to write the decision up) is not
  caught.
- 1.41, 1.63: examples of significant effects: grant or refusal of a benefit under an Act; rights under a
  contract (life insurance); access to a significant service or support (healthcare); targeting content or
  advertising when it leads to differential or personalised pricing for significant goods or services, or
  limits access to employment opportunities. A decision can be caught even with human review, and even where
  the program does not replace the whole process.
- 1.42 to 1.45: "arranged for": the entity responsible for arranging the program carries the duty, including
  a third-party product (AI screening of job applications, an AI chat tool drafting performance assessments,
  a vendor approving refunds, a case system escalating complaints). The duty usually stays with the entity
  using the personal information to decide.
- 1.46, 1.47: "computer program" is broad: rule-based processes, AI, machine learning, generative AI tools,
  chatbots, common software.
- 1.49 to 1.54: "substantially and directly related": a key factor in the human's decision and directly
  connected; advisory outputs can be caught; factors: reliance, likelihood of override, advisory or
  determinative, explainability, integration into the workflow.
- 1.55 to 1.62: "significantly" means more than trivial, with potential to considerably influence the
  individual's circumstances; effects can be adverse or beneficial (1.9(c)); the individual's circumstances
  matter (a child, a vulnerable person); factors: sensitive information, vulnerable persons, intrusive
  practices, significant financial outcomes.
- 1.64 to 1.66: a decision with materially different output for a vulnerable cohort is to be taken as
  significant; OAIC list of significant rights or interests: essential food, healthcare, emergency or aged
  care, physical support, financial assistance, education, essential banking and credit,
  telecommunications, essential utilities, employment opportunities, housing.
- 1.67: balance: meaningful but not excessive detail. Privacy tip: commercial-in-confidence information
  about the systems is excluded (Addendum to the Explanatory Memorandum, p 38); narrow: only information
  whose release could harm commercial interests.
- 1.68 to 1.73: plain language; structured so people can ask for more; specific enough to be meaningful;
  grouped logically; framed so the information and the decision can be challenged. Decisions and kinds of
  information may be grouped, but more sensitive kinds (health information, biometric templates) should be
  clearly identified; one example: a range of fraud prevention tools using identity information,
  geolocation, biometric information and IP addresses to decide whether to permit, flag or deny access to an
  account.
- 1.74 to 1.77: agencies (IPS); not modelled (the generator is for organisations).
- 1.78: further guidance: APP 1.7-1.9 fact sheet, government supplement, flowchart (PDFs). Not read: the
  container cannot reach oaic.gov.au, a web fetch of the PDF failed, and the browser pane disconnected
  while the PDF was parsed in the page. The guideline text above covers the same ground, and the Act is the
  rule; recorded as a gap, not a blocker.
- 1.79, 1.80: optional content: exemptions that apply; who else can access; how changes are publicised;
  criteria and procedure for vulnerable groups such as children; anonymity and pseudonymity (APP 2);
  retention and destruction practices.
- 1.81 to 1.88: APP 1.5 free of charge and in an appropriate form, usually on the website, prominent on each
  page; offline alternatives (premises, printout, a line on correspondence, by telephone); APP 1.6 a copy in
  the form requested by any person or body, reasonable steps, usually free.

## APP Guidelines, Chapter 5 (v1.2, July 2019), read through a web fetch of the PDF

- 5.1, 5.3, 5.4: reasonable steps to notify or ensure awareness; depends on sensitivity, harm, special
  needs, practicability (cost alone is no excuse). 5.7: no steps may be reasonable (already aware, recurring
  collection, danger, would breach another law, burden clearly outweighs benefit); must be justified. 5.8:
  each matter assessed separately.
- 5.34 to 5.38: at or before collection, or as soon as practicable after; build notices into forms.
- 5.5, 5.6, 5.27: layered notices; may point to specific parts of the APP privacy policy (for example the
  overseas disclosure part) after checking it covers this collection, and must alert the person that the
  further information exists; a prominent link to the policy where practicable.
- 5.9: identity and contact details: position title, telephone, email; a generic privacy contact.
- 5.10, 5.11: facts and circumstances of collection, especially from a third party or where the person may
  not know (cookies, RFID, biometrics); name the source or its type.
- 5.12 to 5.14: name the law, instrument or order requiring or authorising collection where practicable,
  or a generic description ("taxation laws").
- 5.15 to 5.17: primary and secondary purposes; internal housekeeping need not be listed.
- 5.18 to 5.20: main consequences only (cannot process an application, reduced service).
- 5.21 to 5.25: usual disclosures: regular, agreed or reasonably predictable; recipients named or by type.
- 5.26, 5.27: (g), (h): that the policy explains access, correction and complaints.
- 5.28 to 5.33: (i), (j): likely overseas disclosure; routing or the entity's own overseas office is a use;
  countries if practicable, appendix or regions otherwise; impracticability needs justifying.

## APP Guidelines, Chapter 8 (v1.3, October 2025), read through a web fetch of the PDF

- 8.6: an overseas office of the same entity is not an overseas recipient; a related body corporate
  overseas is (s 13B does not displace APP 8).
- 8.8 to 8.15: disclosure means giving up effective control; overseas contractors usually a disclosure; a
  cloud provider limited by a binding contract to storage, with the entity keeping effective control, can be
  a use; routing in transit usually a use.
- 8.28 to 8.30: the prescribed country or scheme exception (APP 8.2(aa), 8.3) exists from 11 December 2024;
  the guideline names none and points to the Federal Register.
- 8.31 to 8.37: consent exception: the entity must expressly tell the person, before consent, that APP 8.1
  will not apply if they consent; should explain it will not be accountable under the Act and the person
  cannot seek redress under it; consent informed, voluntary, current, specific; can be withdrawn.
- 8.38 to 8.40: required or authorised by Australian law or an Australian court or tribunal order only;
  a foreign law does not count.
- 8.60 to 8.68: s 16C accountability where APP 8.1 applies; not where an 8.2 exception applies or the
  recipient has an Australian link; s 6A(4) acts required by foreign law done outside Australia.
- 8.67: optional: tell people an overseas recipient may be compelled by foreign law to disclose.

## Regulations, reform and code status (13 November 2026)

- Privacy Regulations 2025 (F2025L01377) replaced the Privacy Regulation 2013 from 1 April 2026 (remake
  without policy change, per the impact analysis notice). Its contents (Parts 1 to 6) deal with
  interpretation, small business operators treated as organisations, State authorities, credit reporting,
  emergencies and APP 9 exceptions. No country or binding scheme is prescribed for APP 8.3. So the
  prescribed-country exception exists in the Act but cannot be used yet: the generator offers it as a
  basis only with a check that the regulations prescribe the country (row fails closed).
- Tranche 2: exposure draft of the Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 released
  31 August 2026, submissions closed 18 September 2026; not introduced as law. Several firm notes (Sparke
  Helmore, Colin Biggers & Paisley) say the draft keeps the small business and employee records exemptions;
  one summary says it revisits them. Not modelled; recorded for the scheduled law check.
- AML/CTF reforms: from 1 July 2026 tranche 2 AML/CTF reporting entities (lawyers, accountants,
  conveyancers, real estate agents, dealers in precious goods) are bound by the Privacy Act for their
  AML/CTF activities even if small (s 6E(1A), per the OAIC and law society notes). The s 6D check names this.
- Children's Online Privacy Code: exposure draft 31 March 2026, consultation closed 5 June 2026; must be
  registered by 10 December 2026; commencement not confirmed (OAIC code page, Corrs, DLA Piper). Not in
  force on 13 November 2026: a check row, not notice text.
- Recognised EDR schemes (s 35A register): AFCA, the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman, the energy and
  water ombudsmen (NSW, Victoria, WA and others), the Public Transport Ombudsman (Victoria), the Tolling
  Customer Ombudsman; ten schemes on the register. The generator asks for the scheme by name (free text).

## Act: second reading (13 November 2026, same compilation No. 104, EPUB HTML fetched in the page)

- s. 6D(4) exactly: not a small business operator if it (a) carries on a business that has had an annual
  turnover over $3,000,000 for a financial year ended after it started the business (or after s. 6D
  commenced); (b) provides a health service and holds any health information except in an employee record;
  (c) discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or
  advantage; (d) provides a benefit, service or advantage to collect personal information about another
  individual from anyone else; (e) is a contracted service provider for a Commonwealth contract; (f) is a
  credit reporting body. (7), (8): (c), (d) do not catch disclosure or collection with consent or as
  required or authorised by legislation. (9): a body corporate related to one carrying on a business that is
  not small is not a small business operator. s. 6DA: annual turnover.
- s. 6E: the Act applies as if it were an organisation to a small business operator that is (1A) an
  AML/CTF reporting entity or its authorised agent (for those activities); (1B) a protected action ballot
  agent (for the ballot); (1C) a registered or recognised association of employees; (1D) accredited under
  the consumer data right (non-CDR personal information); (1), (2) prescribed by regulations. s. 6EA: a
  small business operator can choose in writing to the Commissioner to be treated as an organisation
  (registered; revocable).
- s. 7B(3): an act or practice of an organisation that is or was an employer of an individual is exempt if
  directly related to (a) a current or former employment relationship between them and (b) an employee
  record held by the organisation and relating to the individual. (Job applicants and contractors are not
  covered by these words.) s. 7B(4): journalism.
- s. 5B(1A): the Act extends to acts and practices outside Australia of an organisation or small business
  operator with an Australian link; (2): Australian citizen, a person whose presence is not limited in time,
  a partnership formed, trust created or body corporate incorporated in Australia, an unincorporated
  association managed and controlled there; (3): any other organisation that carries on business in
  Australia or an external Territory.
- s. 16A table: permitted general situations: 1 serious threat to life, health or safety (consent
  unreasonable or impracticable); 2 suspected unlawful activity or serious misconduct; 3 locating a missing
  person (rules); 4 establishment, exercise or defence of a legal or equitable claim; 5 a confidential
  alternative dispute resolution process; 6 agencies (diplomatic or consular); 7 Defence Force. s. 16B:
  permitted health situations (organisations; health services, research, genetic information).
- s. 40(1A): the Commissioner must not investigate a complaint unless the complainant first complained to
  the respondent, unless the Commissioner considers that was not appropriate; (1B) credit reporting
  exceptions.
- s. 6(1) sensitive information: (a) information or an opinion about racial or ethnic origin, political
  opinions, membership of a political association, religious beliefs or affiliations, philosophical
  beliefs, membership of a professional or trade association, membership of a trade union, sexual
  orientation or practices, criminal record; (b) health information; (c) genetic information not otherwise
  health information; (d) biometric information to be used for automated biometric verification or
  identification; (e) biometric templates.
- APP 7.6, 7.7 exactly: where an organisation uses or discloses personal information for its own direct
  marketing or to facilitate others' direct marketing, the person can ask not to receive its direct
  marketing communications, ask it not to use or disclose the information to facilitate others' direct
  marketing, and ask for its source of the information; no charge for making or giving effect to the
  request; given effect within a reasonable period; the source notified within a reasonable period unless
  impracticable or unreasonable. 7.8: does not apply to the extent the Interactive Gambling Act 2001
  (Part 7B Division 5), the Do Not Call Register Act 2006 and the Spam Act 2003 apply.
- APP 12.8: an organisation's charge for giving access must not be excessive and must not apply to making
  the request. 12.9: written reasons and the complaint mechanisms on refusal.
- APP 13.3 to 13.5: written reasons and complaint mechanisms on refusal; a statement associated with the
  information on request; organisations respond within a reasonable period; no charge for the request, the
  correction or the statement.

## APP Guidelines, Chapter B (version 1.4), read in the browser (13 November 2026)

- B.37, B.38: consent is express or implied; four elements: adequately informed, voluntary, current and
  specific, capacity. B.42, B.43: silence or an unexercised opt-out is generally not consent.
- B.51 to B.53: seek consent at collection or at the use or disclosure; not broader than needed.
- B.54: an individual can withdraw consent at any time, and it should be easy and accessible; the entity
  then cannot rely on the past consent for future use or disclosure; tell people the implications (for
  example losing access to a service).
- B.59 to B.61: no fixed age; capacity of under-18s assessed case by case; where that is not practicable,
  an entity can presume a person aged 15 or over has capacity unless something suggests otherwise, and a
  person under 15 does not; a parent or guardian can consent for a young child.

## Act: third reading (APP text checked word by word for the policy wording)

- APP 2.2: no anonymity option where (a) an Australian law or court/tribunal order requires or authorises
  dealing with identified individuals, or (b) it is impracticable to deal with unidentified or pseudonymous
  individuals.
- APP 3.3, 3.4: sensitive information only with consent and reasonably necessary (organisations), or where
  3.4 applies: (a) required or authorised by or under an Australian law or court/tribunal order; (b) a
  permitted general situation; (c) organisations: a permitted health situation; (d) enforcement bodies;
  (e) a non-profit organisation, where the information relates to its activities and solely to its members
  or individuals in regular contact with it in connection with its activities. 3.5 lawful and fair means;
  3.6 from the individual unless unreasonable or impracticable (organisations).
- APP 6.1, 6.2: a secondary purpose only with consent, or (a) reasonably expected and related (directly
  related for sensitive information), (b) required or authorised by Australian law or a court/tribunal
  order, (c) a permitted general situation, (d) organisations: a permitted health situation, (e)
  enforcement related activities.
- APP 11.1: reasonable steps to protect from misuse, interference and loss, and unauthorised access,
  modification or disclosure; 11.2 destroy or de-identify when no longer needed for any permitted purpose,
  unless a Commonwealth record or required by Australian law or a court/tribunal order; 11.3 technical and
  organisational measures.
- APP 12.3 (organisations): access can be refused to the extent of (a) a serious threat to life, health or
  safety, (b) an unreasonable impact on the privacy of others, (c) frivolous or vexatious, (d) legal
  proceedings, (e) negotiations, and further grounds; 12.4 respond within a reasonable period
  (organisations), access in the manner requested if reasonable and practicable; 12.5, 12.6 other means,
  an intermediary.
- APP 13.1: correct where the entity is satisfied the information is inaccurate, out of date, incomplete,
  irrelevant or misleading, or the individual asks; 13.2 notify other APP entities on request.

## After the independent review (13 November 2026)

20 findings (5 high, 8 medium, 7 low), all applied: see `build/review-2026-11-13-au/review-fixes.md`. Re-read for
the fixes, in Compilation No. 104: s. 6C(1) (an organisation is not a small business operator, an agency or a
State or Territory authority), s. 7B(2) (a small contracted service provider is exempt for acts not done for the
Commonwealth contract), ss. 17 and 18 (tax file number rules bind every file number recipient), APP 12.9(a)
(reasons except where unreasonable), APP 13.2 (notification to another APP entity) and 13.3(a). Design changes:
a small business operator with no exception gets a voluntary policy without the OAIC route; permitted general
situations are printed as conditions; employee records are said to be exempt; storage-only providers and own
offices are listed apart from disclosures; the location test accepts a four-digit postcode only after a state,
a territory or "Australia".

# India: reading notes (12 November 2026)

India has one general law: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023, the DPDP
Act), with the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025, the DPDP
Rules). Both were read from MeitY's own copies of the Gazette in the browser pane (pdf.js text, joined by
line), 12 November 2026:

- Act: meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf (Gazette of India,
  Extraordinary, Part II, Section 1, No. 25, 11 August 2023). Read ss. 2 to 17 in full.
- Rules: meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf (41 pages; Hindi
  first, English from page 24). Read Rules 1 to 23 and the First, Second, Third, Fourth and Seventh
  Schedules in full (Fifth and Sixth, on the Board's members and staff, skimmed).
- Corrigenda G.S.R. 892(E), 10 December 2025 (meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/12/
  3c7ebbae0e5456f493f486e6845df86b.pdf): typographical only ("in the Official Gazette", "Departments",
  "given in such order", "every body", "(18 of 2013)", a note's lettering). Nothing changes a notice.
- Commencement G.S.R. 843(E), Board G.S.R. 844(E), Board size G.S.R. 845(E), all 13 November 2025, MeitY
  PDFs c56ceae6..., cc217843..., f6c08379... in static/uploads/2025/11/.

## Commencement (G.S.R. 843(E); Rule 1)
- From 13 November 2025: Act ss. 1(2), 2, 18 to 26, 35, 38 to 43, 44(1) and (3); Rules 1, 2, 17 to 21.
  The Data Protection Board of India was established that day (G.S.R. 844(E)), four members (G.S.R.
  845(E)), head office in the National Capital Region.
- One year later, 13 November 2026: Act s. 6(9) (registration of Consent Managers) and s. 27(1)(d);
  Rule 4 and the First Schedule (Consent Managers).
- Eighteen months later, 13 May 2027: Act ss. 3 to 5, 6(1) to (8) and (10), 7 to 17, 27 (except
  (1)(d)), 28 to 34, 36, 37, 44(2); Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23. Some commentators count to 14 May; the
  notifications say eighteen months from publication, and the Gazette is dated 13 November 2025.
- So the notice duty (s. 5, Rule 3) applies from 13 May 2027. The generator writes the notice now.
- A January 2026 proposal to cut the 18 months to 12 was reported by the press and never notified (law
  firm trackers to October 2026 agree; no amending notification found on MeitY).
- The Digital Personal Data Protection (Removal of Difficulties) Order, 2026, S.O. 5458(E), 5 October
  2026, in force on publication (6 or 7 October 2026): s. 9(1) now reads "of a child or of a person with
  disability who has a lawful guardian"; s. 10(2)(c)(ii) reads "data audit". Read through several law
  firm summaries that quote it the same way; the Gazette copy itself was not read. No change to a notice.

## The Act
- s. 2: (f) child: an individual who has not completed eighteen years. (g) Consent Manager: registered
  with the Board, single point of contact to give, manage, review and withdraw consent through an
  accessible, transparent and interoperable platform. (i) Data Fiduciary: alone or with others determines
  purpose and means. (j) Data Principal: includes the parents or lawful guardian of a child, and the
  lawful guardian of a person with disability acting on her behalf. (k) Data Processor. (t) personal
  data: any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to it. (x) processing:
  wholly or partly automated operations on digital personal data. (za) "specified purpose" means the
  purpose mentioned in the notice given by the Data Fiduciary.
- s. 3: applies to (a) processing of digital personal data within India, collected in digital form or
  in non-digital form and digitised later; (b) processing outside India "if such processing is in
  connection with any activity related to offering of goods or services to Data Principals within the
  territory of India". Not to (c)(i) personal or domestic purposes, (c)(ii) data made publicly available
  by the Data Principal or by a person under a legal obligation to publish it.
- s. 4: process only for a lawful purpose (not expressly forbidden by law) (a) with consent or (b) for
  certain legitimate uses.
- s. 5(1): every request for consent under s. 6 "shall be accompanied or preceded by a notice" informing
  her of (i) the personal data and the purpose for which it is proposed to be processed; (ii) the manner
  in which she may exercise her rights under s. 6(4) and s. 13; (iii) the manner in which she may make a
  complaint to the Board, "in such manner and as may be prescribed". s. 5(2): consent given before
  commencement: notice "as soon as it is reasonably practicable"; processing may continue until consent
  is withdrawn. s. 5(3): "the option to access the contents of the notice ... in English or any language
  specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution".
- s. 6(1): consent free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous with a clear affirmative
  action, for the specified purpose, limited to the personal data necessary for it. (2) any part that
  infringes the Act or another law is invalid. (3) every request for consent in clear and plain language,
  with the English or Eighth Schedule option, "and providing the contact details of a Data Protection
  Officer, where applicable, or of any other person authorised by the Data Fiduciary to respond to any
  communication from the Data Principal for the purpose of exercise of her rights". (4) withdraw at any
  time, "with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which such consent was given". (5)
  consequences of withdrawal borne by the Data Principal; earlier processing stays lawful. (6) within a
  reasonable time, cease and cause Data Processors to cease, unless the Act, the Rules or another law
  requires or authorises processing without consent. (7) to (9) Consent Managers. (10) the Data
  Fiduciary must prove that notice was given and consent obtained.
- s. 7: legitimate uses (a) the specified purpose for which she voluntarily provided the data, where she
  has not indicated she does not consent; (b) the State providing a prescribed subsidy, benefit, service,
  certificate, licence or permit; (c) the State performing a function under law, or sovereignty,
  integrity, security; (d) fulfilling a legal obligation to disclose information to the State; (e)
  complying with a judgment, decree or order (and foreign contractual or civil judgments); (f) medical
  emergency threatening life or health; (g) medical treatment or health services during an epidemic,
  outbreak or other threat to public health; (h) safety or assistance during a disaster or breakdown of
  public order; (i) employment, or safeguarding the employer from loss or liability (corporate espionage,
  trade secrets, intellectual property, classified information), or a service or benefit sought by an
  employee.
- s. 8: (1) responsible for its processors; (2) processors only under a valid contract; (3)
  completeness, accuracy, consistency where data is used for a decision affecting the person or disclosed
  to another Data Fiduciary; (5) reasonable security safeguards; (6) breach intimation to the Board and
  each affected Data Principal; (7) unless retention is necessary for compliance with law, erase on
  withdrawal of consent "or as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer
  being served, whichever is earlier", and cause processors to erase; (8) deemed no longer served after a
  prescribed period without contact or rights exercised; (9) "publish, in such manner as may be
  prescribed, the business contact information of a Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or a person
  who is able to answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary, the questions, if any, raised by the Data
  Principal about the processing of her personal data"; (10) an effective grievance mechanism.
- s. 9: (1) verifiable consent of the parent before processing a child's data, or of the lawful guardian
  of a person with disability; (2) no processing likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being
  of a child; (3) "shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted
  advertising directed at children"; (4) exemptions from (1) and (3) as prescribed; (5) safe-age
  notifications for a verifiably safe Data Fiduciary.
- s. 10: Significant Data Fiduciaries, notified by the Central Government: (2)(a) a DPO who represents
  it, is based in India, answers to the board of directors and is the point of contact for grievance
  redressal; (b) independent data auditor; (c) periodic DPIA and data audit.
- s. 11: right to access, against a Data Fiduciary to whom she has given consent (including under
  s. 7(a)): (a) a summary of personal data processed and the processing activities; (b) the identities
  of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors it was shared with, with a description of the data
  shared; (c) other prescribed information (none prescribed). (2) carve-out for sharing on written
  request of an authority for offences or cyber incidents.
- s. 12: correction, completion, updating and erasure of data she consented to (including s. 7(a)),
  "in accordance with any requirement or procedure under any law"; erasure unless retention is necessary
  for the specified purpose or for compliance with law.
- s. 13: readily available grievance redressal against a Data Fiduciary or Consent Manager; response
  within the prescribed period; (3) "shall exhaust the opportunity of redressing her grievance under this
  section before approaching the Board".
- s. 14: nominate another individual to exercise her rights on death or incapacity (unsoundness of mind
  or infirmity of body).
- s. 15: duties of Data Principals (no impersonation, no false or frivolous complaints, authentic
  information). Not put in the notice.
- s. 16: (1) the Central Government may by notification restrict transfers to a notified country or
  territory (a negative list; none found notified); (2) a law giving higher protection or restricting
  transfer prevails.
- s. 17: (1) Chapter II (except s. 8(1) and (5)), Chapter III and s. 16 do not apply to listed
  processing, including (d) "personal data of Data Principals not within the territory of India is
  processed pursuant to any contract entered into with any person outside the territory of India by any
  person based in India". (3) the Government may notify Data Fiduciaries, including startups, to which
  s. 5, s. 8(3) and (7), ss. 10 and 11 do not apply (none found notified). (4) State processing.

## The Rules
- Rule 3, verbatim: the notice shall "(a) be presented and be understandable independently of any other
  information that has been, is or may be made available by such Data Fiduciary; (b) give, in clear and
  plain language, a fair account of the details necessary to enable the Data Principal to give specific
  and informed consent for the processing of her personal data, which shall include, at the minimum, (i)
  an itemised description of such personal data; and (ii) the specified purpose or purposes of, and
  specific description of the goods or services to be provided or uses to be enabled by, such
  processing; and (c) give, the particular communication link for accessing the website or app, or both,
  of such Data Fiduciary, and a description of other means, if any, using which such Data Principal may
  (i) withdraw her consent, with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent
  was given; (ii) exercise her rights under the Act; and (iii) make a complaint to the Board."
- Rule 4 and First Schedule: Consent Managers (company incorporated in India, net worth at least two
  crore rupees, records of consents and notices kept seven years). In force 13 November 2026.
- Rule 7: breach intimation (not notice content).
- Rule 8 and Third Schedule: (1) e-commerce entities with at least two crore registered users in India,
  online gaming intermediaries with at least fifty lakh, and social media intermediaries with at least
  two crore, erase data three years after the Data Principal last approached them for the specified
  purpose or exercised her rights, or after the Rules commenced, whichever is latest (except to access a
  user account or a stored virtual token); (2) "at least forty-eight hours before completion of the time
  period for erasure", inform her that the data will be erased unless she logs in or otherwise initiates
  contact or exercises her rights; (3) every Data Fiduciary retains personal data, associated traffic
  data and other logs of the processing "for a minimum period of one year from the date of such
  processing, for the purposes as specified in the Seventh Schedule", then erases them unless another
  law requires longer.
- Rule 9, verbatim: "Every Data Fiduciary shall prominently publish on its website or app, and mention in
  every response to a communication for the exercise of the rights of a Data Principal under the Act, the
  business contact information of the Data Protection Officer, if applicable, or a person who is able to
  answer on behalf of the Data Fiduciary the questions of the Data Principal about the processing of her
  personal data."
- Rule 10: verifiable consent of the parent; due diligence that the person identifying as parent is an
  identifiable adult (18 or over) by reference to (a) reliable identity and age details already held, or
  (b) details voluntarily provided by the individual or through a virtual token issued by an authorised
  entity (including a Digital Locker service provider).
- Rule 11: for a lawful guardian of a person with disability, verify that a court, a designated
  authority (Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, s. 15) or a local level committee (National
  Trust Act, 1999, s. 13) appointed the guardian.
- Rule 12 and Fourth Schedule: s. 9(1) and (3) do not apply to Part A classes (clinical and mental
  health establishments and healthcare professionals, allied healthcare professionals, educational
  institutions, creches and child day care, transport for them) and Part B purposes (functions under law
  in the interests of a child, State subsidies under s. 7(b), an email-only user account, real-time
  location for safety, keeping harmful content or advertising away from a child, confirming that a user
  is not a child), each within its conditions.
- Rule 13: Significant Data Fiduciaries: yearly DPIA and audit, algorithmic due diligence, localisation
  of data the Government specifies.
- Rule 14: (1) publish prominently on the website or app (a) the details of the means for requests to
  exercise rights and (b) the particulars, such as a username or other identifier, needed to identify
  her; (2) she makes the request to the Data Fiduciary she consented to, using those means; (3) as
  printed: "Every Data Fiduciary and Consent Manager shall prominently publish on its website or app, or
  both, as the case may be, within a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days under its grievance
  redressal system for responding to the grievances of Data Principals and shall ... implement
  appropriate technical and organisational measures." Words are missing (the corrigendum does not fix
  them). Read with s. 13(2): grievances are answered within a period the Data Fiduciary publishes, no
  longer than ninety days. MeitY's press release of 14 November 2025 (PIB, PRID 2190014, read 12 November
  2026) applies the same ceiling to requests to exercise rights: "Data Fiduciaries must respond to all such
  requests within a maximum of 90 days". The same release says the Board will let citizens file and track
  complaints online "through a dedicated platform and mobile app". (4) she can nominate
  one or more individuals using the means the Data Fiduciary requires; (5) "identifier" includes customer
  numbers, application numbers, enrolment IDs, email, mobile number, licence number.
- Rule 15: data "may be transferred outside the territory of India" subject to requirements the
  Government may specify by order for making it available to a foreign State or entities it controls.
- Rule 16 and Second Schedule: research, archiving or statistics exempt if the standards are met.
- Rule 20: the Board functions as a digital office. Rule 22: appeals to the Telecom Disputes Settlement
  and Appellate Tribunal. Rule 23 and Seventh Schedule: the Government can call for information
  (sovereignty and security; State functions under law; assessment for Significant Data Fiduciary
  status).

## What the notice therefore needs (Part P)
Contents: itemised personal data per purpose; the purpose and the goods, services or uses it enables;
the link to the website or app, and other means, to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the
Board; the contact of the DPO or the person who answers questions; the option to read the notice in
English or an Eighth Schedule language. Rights to put in the notice: access (s. 11), correction,
completion, updating and erasure (s. 12), withdrawal (s. 6(4)), grievance redressal (s. 13), nomination
(s. 14). The grievance step comes before a complaint to the Board. Erasure rules (s. 8(7), Rule 8) are
stated because they change what people can expect. Processing outside India is not a notice element under
the Act; it is shown when the user says it happens.

## Open points
- The Board's complaint route: the Board works as a digital office (Rule 20) but no official complaint
  page was found; the generator asks for the link and leaves a placeholder. The reviewer found reports
  (August and September 2026, secondary) that no Chairperson or Members had been appointed; MeitY invited
  applications on 6 May 2026.
- Rule 14(3) as printed is missing words: read as a published response period of at most 90 days.
- The Removal of Difficulties Order was read only through law firm summaries.
- The constitutional challenge to the Act before the Supreme Court (reported by secondary sources) is not
  modelled.

## Review (12 November 2026)
22 findings (3 high, 6 medium, 13 low), all applied: see `review-2026-11-12-in/review-fixes.md`. The
design changes: tracking and targeted advertising are separate questions (no Fourth Schedule item covers
advertising); a new question asks whether all processing of children's data is within an exemption;
section 7(a) is not offered for a new purpose and cannot carry data from another source; access,
correction and erasure are listed only where a purpose rests on consent or section 7(a); the Rule 8(3)
one-year minimum is checked against the retention periods.

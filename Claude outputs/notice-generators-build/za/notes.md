### s. 1 Definitions (as read)
- child: natural person under 18 who is not legally competent, without the assistance of a competent person,
  to take any action or decision about him- or herself. competent person: legally competent to consent for a child.
- consent: any voluntary, specific and informed expression of will by which permission is given for processing.
- data subject: the person to whom personal information relates (natural or juristic: "person" = natural or
  juristic person).
- direct marketing: approaching a data subject in person, by mail or electronic communication, directly or
  indirectly to (a) promote or offer goods or services in the ordinary course of business, or (b) request a donation.
- electronic communication: text, voice, sound or image message sent over an electronic communications network,
  stored in the network or terminal until collected.
- information officer: public body, the information officer or deputy under PAIA s. 1 or 17; private body, the
  head of a private body under PAIA s. 1.
- operator: processes personal information for a responsible party under a contract or mandate, without coming
  under its direct authority.
- personal information: about an identifiable, living, natural person and, where applicable, an identifiable,
  existing juristic person; list (a) to (h) includes race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, origin, colour,
  sexual orientation, age, health, disability, religion, belief, culture, language, birth; education, medical,
  financial, criminal, employment history; identifying number, email, address, phone, location, online
  identifier; biometric information; opinions; private correspondence; views of others; name with other info.
- private body: (a) natural person carrying on trade, business or profession, in that capacity; (b) partnership;
  (c) any former or existing juristic person, excluding a public body.
- public body: (a) department of state or administration in national or provincial sphere, or a municipality;
  (b) other functionary or institution exercising a constitutional power or public power under legislation.
- responsible party: public or private body or any other person that, alone or with others, determines the
  purpose of and means for processing.
- special personal information: as in s. 26. unique identifier: assigned and used by the responsible party,
  uniquely identifies the data subject to it.
- "this Act" includes any regulation or code of conduct made under it. "prescribed": by regulation or code.

### s. 3 Application
- (1) Processing of personal information entered in a record by or for a responsible party (automated, or
  non-automated forming part of a filing system) where the responsible party is (i) domiciled in the Republic, or
  (ii) not domiciled there but makes use of automated or non-automated means in the Republic, unless only to
  forward personal information through the Republic.
- (2) Prevails over materially inconsistent legislation; more extensive conditions in other legislation prevail.

### s. 4 Conditions
- Eight conditions: accountability (s. 8), processing limitation (ss. 9 to 12), purpose specification (13, 14),
  further processing limitation (15), information quality (16), openness (17, 18), security safeguards (19 to 22),
  data subject participation (23 to 25).
- Special personal information prohibited (s. 26) unless ss. 27 to 33 or a Regulator authorisation (s. 27(2)).
  Children's personal information prohibited (s. 34) unless s. 35(1) or authorisation (s. 35(2)).
- (6) Direct marketing by any means: Chapter 3 read with s. 69 for unsolicited electronic communications.

### s. 5 Rights of data subjects (the list a policy can reproduce)
(a) to be notified that personal information is being collected (s. 18) or has been accessed or acquired by an
unauthorised person (s. 22); (b) to establish whether a responsible party holds personal information and to
request access (s. 23); (c) to request, where necessary, correction, destruction or deletion (s. 24); (d) to object,
on reasonable grounds relating to the particular situation, under s. 11(3)(a); (e) to object at any time to direct
marketing (s. 11(3)(b)) or under s. 69(3)(c); (f) not to have personal information processed for direct marketing by
unsolicited electronic communications except under s. 69(1); (g) not to be subject, under certain circumstances, to
a decision based solely on automated processing intended to provide a profile (s. 71); (h) to submit a complaint to
the Regulator (s. 74), including about an adjudicator's determination; (i) to institute civil proceedings (s. 99).

### s. 6, 7 Exclusions
- s. 6(1): purely personal or household activity; de-identified so it cannot be re-identified; public body for
  national security, defence, public safety, or crime and money laundering, to the extent of adequate legislated
  safeguards; Cabinet and Executive Councils; judicial functions of courts.
- s. 7: solely journalistic, literary or artistic expression, to the extent necessary.

### Condition 1 to 4 (ss. 8 to 15)
- s. 8: responsible party ensures compliance at determination of purpose and means and during processing.
- s. 9: lawfully and in a reasonable manner that does not infringe privacy. s. 10: adequate, relevant, not excessive.
- s. 11(1) justification (the "lawful bases"): (a) consent of the data subject, or of a competent person where the
  data subject is a child; (b) necessary to carry out actions for the conclusion or performance of a contract to which
  the data subject is party; (c) complies with an obligation imposed by law on the responsible party; (d) protects a
  legitimate interest of the data subject; (e) necessary for the proper performance of a public law duty by a public
  body; (f) necessary for pursuing the legitimate interests of the responsible party or of a third party to whom the
  information is supplied.
- s. 11(2): (a) burden of proof of consent on the responsible party; (b) consent can be withdrawn at any time; lawfulness
  before withdrawal, and processing under (1)(b) to (f), not affected.
- s. 11(3): object at any time (a) to processing under (1)(d) to (f), in the prescribed manner, on reasonable grounds
  relating to the particular situation, unless legislation provides for the processing; (b) for direct marketing
  other than by unsolicited electronic communications (s. 69). s. 11(4): after an objection, no further processing.
- s. 12: collect directly from the data subject, unless (2)(a) public record or deliberately made public, (b) consent
  to collection from another source, (c) no prejudice to a legitimate interest of the data subject, (d) necessary for
  law enforcement, a legal obligation or revenue, court proceedings, national security, or the legitimate interests of
  the responsible party or a third party, (e) compliance would prejudice a lawful purpose, (f) not reasonably practicable.
- s. 13: specific, explicitly defined and lawful purpose related to a function or activity; (2) awareness under s. 18(1)
  unless s. 18(4).
- s. 14: retention no longer than necessary unless (a) required or authorised by law, (b) reasonably required for lawful
  purposes related to functions or activities, (c) required by a contract between the parties, (d) consent; (2) longer for
  historical, statistical or research purposes with safeguards; (3) a record used to make a decision kept as long as law
  or a code prescribes, or else long enough for the data subject to request access; (4), (5) destroy, delete or
  de-identify as soon as reasonably practicable, in a way that prevents reconstruction; (6) to (8) restriction (accuracy
  contested, kept for proof, unlawful processing and the data subject asks restriction, or a request to transmit the
  personal data into another automated processing system); inform before lifting a restriction.
- s. 15: further processing compatible with the purpose of collection; factors (2)(a) to (e); not incompatible if
  (3)(a) consent, (b) public record or made public, (c) law enforcement, legal obligation or revenue, court
  proceedings, national security, (d) serious and imminent threat to public health or safety or to life or health,
  (e) historical, statistical or research and not published identifiably, (f) a s. 37 exemption.

### Condition 6 Openness (ss. 17, 18): the notice duty
- s. 17: maintain documentation of all processing operations as referred to in PAIA s. 14 or 51 (the PAIA manual).
- s. 18(1): if personal information is collected, take reasonably practicable steps to ensure the data subject is aware of:
  (a) the information being collected and, where not collected from the data subject, the source;
  (b) the name and address of the responsible party;
  (c) the purpose for which the information is being collected;
  (d) whether or not the supply of the information by that data subject is voluntary or mandatory;
  (e) the consequences of failure to provide the information;
  (f) any particular law authorising or requiring the collection of the information;
  (g) the fact that, where applicable, the responsible party intends to transfer the information to a third country or
      international organisation and the level of protection afforded to the information by that third country or
      international organisation;
  (h) any further information such as the (i) recipient or category of recipients; (ii) nature or category of the
      information; (iii) existence of the right of access to and the right to rectify the information collected;
      (iv) existence of the right to object under s. 11(3); and (v) right to lodge a complaint to the Information
      Regulator and the contact details of the Information Regulator, which is necessary, having regard to the specific
      circumstances, to enable processing in respect of the data subject to be reasonable.
- s. 18(2): (a) collected directly: before the information is collected, unless the data subject is already aware;
  (b) otherwise: before, or as soon as reasonably practicable after, collection.
- s. 18(3): earlier steps cover later collection of the same kind of information for the same purpose.
- s. 18(4): not necessary if (a) consent to non-compliance (data subject or competent person), (b) no prejudice to the
  data subject's legitimate interests, (c) law enforcement, legal obligation or revenue, court proceedings, national
  security, (d) compliance would prejudice a lawful purpose, (e) not reasonably practicable, (f) information not used in
  an identifiable form, or used for historical, statistical or research purposes. Documented, not offered as an opt-out.

### Condition 7 Security (ss. 19 to 22)
- s. 19: appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures; identify risks, safeguards, verify, update; have
  regard to generally accepted information security practices. s. 20, 21: operators under a written contract; operator
  notifies the responsible party immediately of a compromise.
- s. 22: notify the Regulator and the data subject (unless identity cannot be established) as soon as reasonably
  possible; in writing by mail, email, a prominent website position, news media or as the Regulator directs; content
  (5)(a) to (d). Breach notification is not policy content (not modelled; delivery row only).

### Condition 8 Data subject participation (ss. 23 to 25)
- s. 23(1): with adequate proof of identity, (a) confirm free of charge whether personal information is held; (b) the
  record or a description, including the identity of all third parties or categories of third parties who have or have
  had access, within a reasonable time, at a prescribed fee if any, in a reasonable manner and format, in a generally
  understandable form. (2) Advise of the right to request correction. (3) A fee: written estimate first, deposit possible.
  (4) Refusal grounds of PAIA Chapter 4 of Part 2 (public bodies) and Part 3 (private bodies); PAIA ss. 30 and 61 for
  health records. (5) Severability.
- s. 24(1): in the prescribed manner, request (a) correction or deletion of information that is inaccurate, irrelevant,
  excessive, out of date, incomplete, misleading or obtained unlawfully, or (b) destruction or deletion of a record no
  longer authorised to be retained. (2) As soon as reasonably practicable: correct, destroy or delete, give credible
  evidence, or attach an indication that a correction was requested but not made. (3) Inform those to whom it was
  disclosed if the change affects decisions, if reasonably practicable. (4) Notify the data subject of the action taken.
- s. 25: PAIA ss. 18 (form of request) and 53 apply to s. 23 requests.

### Special personal information (ss. 26 to 33)
- s. 26: religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion,
  health or sex life, biometric information; criminal behaviour as to alleged offences or proceedings.
- s. 27(1) general authorisation: (a) consent; (b) necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of a right or
  obligation in law; (c) necessary to comply with an obligation of international public law; (d) historical,
  statistical or research purposes (public interest and necessary, or consent impossible or disproportionate) with
  sufficient guarantees; (e) deliberately made public by the data subject; (f) ss. 28 to 33 complied with.
  (2), (3) Regulator authorisation in the public interest, by Gazette notice, with conditions.
- s. 28 religious or philosophical beliefs: spiritual or religious organisations (members, or to achieve aims); institutions
  founded on religious or philosophical principles (members, employees); other institutions to protect spiritual welfare
  unless objected; family members (regular contact, no written objection); no supply to third parties without consent.
- s. 29 race or ethnic origin: to identify data subjects where essential, and to comply with laws or measures to protect or
  advance persons disadvantaged by unfair discrimination.
- s. 30 trade union membership: by the trade union or its federation, to achieve its aims; no supply to third parties
  without consent.
- s. 31 political persuasion: institutions founded on political principles (members, employees); forming a party,
  participating in or recruiting for a party for elections or referendums, campaigning.
- s. 32 health or sex life: (1)(a) medical professionals, healthcare institutions or facilities, social services, for
  proper treatment and care or administration; (b) insurers, medical schemes, administrators, managed healthcare, for
  assessing the risk (and the data subject has not objected), performance of the agreement, enforcement of contractual
  rights; (c) schools, special support; (d) bodies managing the care of a child; (e) public bodies for prison sentences
  or detention; (f) administrative bodies, pension funds, employers, for laws, pension regulations or collective
  agreements creating rights dependent on health, or reintegration or support of workers for sickness or work
  incapacity. (2), (3) Confidentiality duty. (4) Supplementing health processing. (5) Inherited characteristics only for a
  serious medical interest or research.
- s. 33 criminal behaviour or biometric information: by bodies charged by law with applying criminal law, or by
  responsible parties that obtained it in accordance with the law; (2) personnel information under labour legislation.

### Children (ss. 34, 35)
- s. 34: no processing of personal information concerning a child (under 18), subject to s. 35.
- s. 35(1): (a) prior consent of a competent person; (b) establishment, exercise or defence of a right or obligation
  in law; (c) obligation of international public law; (d) historical, statistical or research purposes (public interest,
  or consent impossible or disproportionate) with sufficient guarantees; (e) deliberately made public by the child with
  the consent of a competent person. (2), (3) Regulator authorisation, with conditions such as notice of the nature of
  children's information, how it is processed and further processing practices.
- Note: no age of digital consent below 18; a child under 18 needs a competent person (s. 1). The child definition's
  "who is not legally competent ... without the assistance of a competent person" qualifier is read by the Regulator as
  the under-18 rule (to check in the Regulator's guidance on children).

### Chapter 4 exemptions (ss. 36 to 38): documented, not offered
- s. 37 Regulator exemptions by Gazette notice; s. 38 relevant functions (protecting the public against financial loss
  or malpractice) exempt from ss. 11(3), (4), 12, 15 and 18 to the extent of prejudice.

### Information Officer (ss. 55, 56)
- s. 55(1): encourage compliance, deal with requests, work with the Regulator, ensure compliance. (2) Officers take up
  their duties only after the responsible party has registered them with the Regulator.
- s. 56: deputy information officers designated as in PAIA s. 17.
- For a private body the information officer is the head of the body (PAIA s. 1: the chief executive officer or
  equivalent, or a person authorised by them; for a natural person, that person). Policy content: the name and contact
  details of the information officer are good practice (and required in the PAIA manual).

### Prior authorisation (ss. 57 to 59)
- s. 57(1) before processing: (a) unique identifiers for another purpose with the aim of linking with other responsible
  parties' information; (b) criminal behaviour or unlawful or objectionable conduct on behalf of third parties;
  (c) credit reporting; (d) transferring special personal information or children's personal information to a third
  party in a foreign country without an adequate level of protection under s. 72. (3) Not if a code of conduct for the
  sector is in force. s. 58(1), (2) notify and wait (s. 58(2) in force 1 July 2021). A check row, not policy content.

### Codes of conduct (ss. 60 to 68)
- s. 60: issued by the Regulator; incorporate all conditions or a functional equivalent; by information, body, activity
  or industry; must specify measures for s. 71 automated decisions. Which codes are issued: to check (Regulator site).

### Chapter 8: direct marketing, directories, automated decisions (ss. 69 to 71)
- s. 69(1): direct marketing by any form of electronic communication (automatic calling machines, fax, SMS, email)
  prohibited unless the data subject (a) consented, or (b) is, subject to (3), a customer.
- s. 69(2): approach a data subject whose consent is needed and who has not withheld it only once to request consent;
  in the prescribed manner and form (Form 4 of the Regulations).
- s. 69(3): customers only if (a) contact details obtained in the context of the sale of a product or service, (b) for
  marketing the responsible party's own similar products or services, (c) a reasonable opportunity to object, free of
  charge and without unnecessary formality, (i) at collection and (ii) on each communication if not initially refused.
- s. 69(4): every direct marketing communication contains (a) the identity of the sender or the person on whose behalf
  it is sent, and (b) an address or other contact details for a request that the communications cease.
- s. 70: subscriber directories: inform free of charge before inclusion about the purpose and further uses (search
  functions); opportunity to object. Not modelled (directory publishers only).
- s. 71(1): no decision with legal consequences or substantial effect based solely on automated processing intended to
  provide a profile (performance at work, credit worthiness, reliability, location, health, personal preferences or
  conduct). (2) Unless (a) in connection with the conclusion or execution of a contract and (i) the data subject's request
  under the contract was met, or (ii) appropriate measures protect the data subject's legitimate interests; or (b) governed
  by a law or code of conduct specifying appropriate measures. (3) The (2)(a)(ii) measures must (a) give an opportunity to
  make representations about the decision, and (b) require sufficient information about the underlying logic to enable
  representations.

### Transborder flows (s. 72)
- s. 72(1): a responsible party in the Republic may not transfer personal information to a third party in a foreign
  country unless (a) the recipient is subject to a law, binding corporate rules or binding agreement providing an
  adequate level of protection that (i) effectively upholds principles substantially similar to the conditions (for
  natural and, where applicable, juristic persons) and (ii) includes onward-transfer provisions substantially similar to
  s. 72; (b) the data subject consents; (c) necessary for the performance of a contract between the data subject and the
  responsible party, or pre-contractual measures at the data subject's request; (d) necessary for the conclusion or
  performance of a contract concluded in the interest of the data subject between the responsible party and a third
  party; (e) for the benefit of the data subject, consent not reasonably practicable, and the data subject would be
  likely to give it.
- s. 72(2): binding corporate rules within a group of undertakings (controlling undertaking and its controlled ones).
- No adequacy list exists in the Act; the responsible party assesses (a). s. 57(1)(d): prior authorisation for special
  personal information or children's information to a country without adequate protection.
- Notice link: s. 18(1)(g) requires, where applicable, the intention to transfer to a third country or international
  organisation and the level of protection afforded there.
- Scope note: s. 72 binds "a responsible party in the Republic"; a responsible party not domiciled but using means in
  the Republic is under the Act (s. 3(1)(b)(ii)) but s. 72 speaks of a responsible party in the Republic: record as
  not modelled for organisations abroad (as for Singapore).

### Enforcement (ss. 73 to 75, 99)
- s. 73: interference = breach of a Chapter 3 condition; non-compliance with ss. 22, 54, 69, 70, 71 or 72; breach of a
  code of conduct.
- s. 74(1): any person may submit a complaint to the Regulator in the prescribed manner and form; (2) about an
  adjudicator's determination. s. 75: in writing; the Regulator gives reasonable assistance.
- s. 99: civil action for damages by the data subject or, at its request, the Regulator, whether or not there is intent
  or negligence; defences (a) to (e).
- Note: the Act does not require a complaint to the responsible party first; the 2025 Regulations (to check) and the
  Regulator's complaint form.

### PAIA s. 51 manual (as amended by POPIA s. 110 and the Schedule, item 12; in force 30 June 2021)
- The head of a private body makes a manual available with (c) for POPIA: (i) the purpose of the processing; (ii) a
  description of the categories of data subjects and of the information or categories of information; (iii) the
  recipients or categories of recipients to whom the personal information may be supplied; (iv) planned transborder
  flows of personal information; (v) a general description allowing a preliminary assessment of the suitability of the
  information security measures. Made available (3) on the website, at the principal place of business, to any person
  on request for a reasonable amount, and to the Regulator on request. Public bodies: PAIA s. 14(1)(c), same items, in
  at least three official languages. A delivery row: the policy does not replace the PAIA manual. (Exemptions for small
  private bodies from compiling the manual: to check.)

(25 pages: the tabling note signed 21 January 2025, the Schedule, Forms 1 to 5). The Gazette notice (gov.za

- reg 2 (substituted): 2.1, 2.2 objections under s. 11(3)(a) and (b) submitted to the responsible party at any time during

  subject; **2.4 a responsible party must, when collecting personal information, notify the data subject, under
  s. 18(1)(h)(iv), of their right to object as referred to in s. 11(3)** (so the right to object is a mandatory notice item,

- reg 6 (substituted): 6.1 direct marketing through unsolicited electronic communication requires written consent under

  calling machine consent requests recorded, recording and transcription free on request; **6.4 for direct marketing
  through unsolicited electronic communications, opt-out does not constitute consent under s. 69(2).**
- reg 7: who may complain (7.1: the data subject, a person acting for the data subject, a person with a sufficient
  personal interest, a responsible party or data subject aggrieved by an adjudicator's determination, a person acting in
  the public interest); 7.2 in writing on the online Form 5 on the Regulator's website or the Form 5 at the Regulator's
  offices; 7.3 the Regulator assists to reduce a complaint to writing (another language than English where possible); 7.5
  also by fax, post, courier, or email to a designated address; 7.6 acknowledgement and reference number within 14 days;
  7.7 to 7.9 contents; 7.10, 7.11 identity protection; 7.12 free assistance.
- reg 12: Forms 17 to 19 deleted. New administrative fines rule (9.1, 9.2): instalments case by case.
- Forms 1 (objection, now with reasons under s. 11(3)(a)), 2 (correction etc. with the seven reasons), 3, 4 (consent for
  direct marketing: goods or services to be marketed, preferred method fax, email, SMS, other), 5 (complaint).
- Generator consequences: the right to object (s. 11(3)) is always printed when any purpose rests on s. 11(1)(d) to (f)
  or direct marketing; the routes for objections and requests must be free of charge and accessible by hand, fax, post,
  email, SMS or WhatsApp or any expedient manner; telephone requests recorded; correction outcome within 30 days in writing.

## Information Regulator guidance (advisory; read in the browser pane on inforegulator.org.za with pdf.js)

### Guidance Note on Information Officers and Deputy Information Officers (1 April 2021; file uploaded July 2025)
- Head of a private body (PAIA s. 1): a natural person or a person duly authorised by them; a partnership, any partner or a
  person authorised; a juristic person, the chief executive officer or equivalent, or a person duly authorised. PAIA allows
  the information officer of a private body to authorise any person as information officer (2.2).
- Information officers and deputies take up duties only after registration with the Regulator (s. 55(2)); registration through
  the eServices portal. 12: "In order to ensure accessibility of a body, the Regulator will make the contact details of the
  Information Officers and that of his or her Deputy Information Officer(s) available on its website."
- Policy consequence: name the information officer (or the title) with contact details; warn if not registered.

### Guidance Note on Direct Marketing (undated; links tested 14 November 2024, so issued late 2024 or later)
- Two types: (4.1.1) other than unsolicited electronic communication (post, hand-delivered mail, in person, letterbox drops):
  s. 11 bases, consent or legitimate interests (s. 11(1)(d) or (f)) after a three-stage legitimate interest assessment
  (purpose, necessity, balancing) done before processing (5.2); objection at any time under s. 11(3)(b), on Form 1 or similar,
  free of charge; processing stops (s. 11(4)); keep a database of objections (6.4).
- (4.1.2, 7.1) Unsolicited electronic communication includes telephone, email, SMS, automatic calling machines, fax, push
  notifications, direct messaging on Instagram or LinkedIn, and the use of cookies. 7.1.1: the Regulator's view is that
  telephone calling is electronic communication (VoIP stores voice packets on the network).
- 7.2 Not a customer: consent first; the first communication must be the request for consent; only once, and not if consent
  was previously withheld; written consent on Form 4 or similar specifying the goods or services and the preferred method;
  telephone: read Form 4 and record; automatic calling machine: record. Burden of proof on the responsible party; withdrawal
  at any time.
- 7.3 Customers: contact details obtained in the context of a sale; own similar products or services (clothing store: shoes,
  belts; funeral insurance is not similar); a reasonable opportunity to object at collection and on each communication; silence
  is not consent. 7.3.4 Every direct marketing communication (s. 11 and s. 69) contains the sender's identity and an address
  or contact details to stop. 7.3.5 database of withheld consent and objections.
- 8: the Consumer Protection Act s. 11 pre-emptive block registry does not replace s. 69 consent.
- 10.1 Lead generation: sharing or selling contact lists is further processing (s. 15) and needs s. 18 notification.
  10.2, 10.3 s. 71 applies only when all three are present (legal or substantial effect, solely automated, profile).
- 11: advisory; the Act and Regulations prevail.
- Policy consequences: direct marketing by electronic means (incl. phone calls and cookies, per the Regulator) on consent or
  the customer exception, never on legitimate interests; non-electronic direct marketing may rest on s. 11(1)(d) or (f) with a
  documented assessment, with the right to object printed.

### Complaints: the Act and the Regulator's Rules of Procedure (October 2021)
- s. 77(1): the Regulator may take no action if, among others, (f) a code of conduct with a complaints procedure is in force and
  the complainant did not pursue it reasonably. The Act does not require a complaint to the responsible party first.
- Rules of procedure for handling POPIA complaints (October 2021, 37 pages, read in the browser pane): who may complain (4.1),
  in writing on the online form or Form 5 (4.2), at offices, by fax, post, courier or email (4.3), acknowledgement within 14
  days (4.3.5); the Regulator does not accept complaints whose cause of action arose before 1 July 2021 (4.5.2), may not
  accept complaints pending elsewhere (4.5.3) or lodged more than three years after the subject matter arose (4.5.4, with
  condonation factors); internal review on Form 20 within 14 days. No rule requiring a complaint to the responsible party
  first. The generator says people may complain to the organisation and to the Regulator, without making the first a
  condition.

## Part P. India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025

Added 12 November 2026. Row IDs start with I. How each text was read, and what it says, is in
`build/in/notes.md`.

**Sources.** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023), MeitY's copy of the Gazette
of 11 August 2023: ss. 2 to 17 read in full. The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R.
846(E), 13 November 2025), MeitY's copy of the Gazette: Rules 1 to 23 and the First, Second, Third,
Fourth and Seventh Schedules read in full. Corrigenda G.S.R. 892(E) of 10 December 2025 (typographical
only). Commencement G.S.R. 843(E), Board G.S.R. 844(E) and G.S.R. 845(E), all 13 November 2025. MeitY's
press release of 14 November 2025 (PIB) for the 90-day ceiling and the Board's online complaints. The
Removal of Difficulties Order, 2026 (S.O. 5458(E), 5 October 2026) through law firm summaries only; it
changes the wording of ss. 9(1) and 10(2)(c)(ii), and nothing in a notice.

**Commencement.** The notice duty (s. 5; Rule 3) and the rights (ss. 11 to 14) apply from 13 May 2027.
Consent Managers register from 13 November 2026 (s. 6(9); Rule 4). The Board exists since 13 November
2025. A proposal to shorten the 18 months was reported in January 2026 and never notified.

**Why this structure.** The DPDP Act has no legal bases as the GDPR has them: a purpose rests on consent
(s. 6) or on one of the legitimate uses in s. 7, which need no consent. The notice is the one that goes
with or before a request for consent (s. 5(1)); Rule 3 sets its minimum: an itemised description of the
personal data, the specified purpose with the goods, services or uses it enables, and the link to the
website or app, with other means, to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board. An India-
led notice reuses the shared answers (purposes, data, retention, sources, recipients) and adds per purpose
the ground and the goods or services enabled. The rest sits in an India section of the form: how the Act
applies, the contact person (the DPO of a Significant Data Fiduciary), the link and other means, the
identifier needed, the response period, the Board, languages, Consent Manager, children and persons with
disability, the Third Schedule class, and processing outside India. With the EU, UK, Swiss, Brazilian or
Canadian law leading, India adds an "Additional information for people in India" section. India sits after
Canada and before California in the order of laws.

In an India-led notice the WP260 delivery rows (D01, D03, D05 to D08, D12, D14) stay as good practice,
and P19 (joint processing) and P21 (first layer) are kept as good practice, as in a Canada-led notice.
Automated decisions are shown when the user answers Yes, with the s. 8(3) accuracy duty; the Act has no
rule of its own on them.

Not modelled: s. 17 exemptions other than the s. 17(1)(d) warning; State processing under s. 7(b) and (c)
beyond a warning; the duties of Data Principals (s. 15); breach intimation (s. 8(6); Rule 7); translations
into the Eighth Schedule languages.

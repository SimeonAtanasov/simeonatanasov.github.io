## Part J. United Kingdom: UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018

Added 12 November 2026. Read on legislation.gov.uk, versions in force that day: UK GDPR as
amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (Article 13 shows versions of 5 February 2026,
19 June 2026 and 30 September 2026), and the Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA). The UK GDPR keeps
the GDPR's article numbers, so rows P01, P03 to P08, P10 to P13, P15, P16 and P18 to P21 apply
to a UK-led notice unchanged, cited as UK GDPR. Rows P02, P09, P14 and P17 are EU-only; J01, J04,
J06 and J08 take their place.

**How regimes work.** The privacy notice form has a "Laws the notice covers" field: EU GDPR,
UK GDPR, Swiss FADP. The first ticked in that order leads and sets the main text; each other
one adds an "Additional information for people in ..." section with its own rows. Nothing
ticked reads as the EU GDPR, which keeps every notice saved before this change identical.
Each law has its own representative and transfer fields, and answers written for one law (the
Schedule 1 condition, the law behind a legal obligation, a restriction, the compatibility of a
new purpose) keep one field per leading law, so changing which law leads never moves an answer
from one law to another. Section openings: the UK section says the rest of the notice is read
with the UK GDPR in place of the GDPR, that it prevails where it differs and that its complaint
route replaces the main one; the Swiss section says the GDPR rights, objection and complaint
sections apply only where the GDPR applies. A controller abroad always says where it processes
the data; transfer rows list onward transfers only, and their recipients must also be in the
recipients list (P08, K04).

**UK points documented, not generated.** 13(4) "to the extent that"; 13(5) to (7) exempt further
processing for research, archiving or statistics under Art. 84B where informing is impossible
or disproportionate, with public information instead; 14(5)(e), (f), (6), (7) the moved
disproportionate effort exemption, also with public information. Art. 8A sets compatibility of
further processing (Art. 6(4) is omitted in the UK GDPR); the further processing field is
labelled accordingly. Art. 6(11) lists examples of legitimate interests (direct marketing,
intra-group administration, network security) but they still need the (f) balancing test.
DPA Schedules 2 to 4 exemptions are recorded, not tested. The Age Appropriate Design Code is
not modelled.

## Part K. Switzerland: Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP)

Added 12 November 2026. FADP, SR 235.1, version of 7 July 2025, and Data Protection Ordinance
(DPO), SR 235.11 (Art. 8 amended 1 December 2025), both read on fedlex. The English text has no
legal force; German, French and Italian are authoritative.

**Why a Swiss-led notice looks different.** Private processing needs no legal basis under the
FADP; it is lawful unless it breaches personality rights without a justification (Arts. 30,
31). Article 19 asks for less than the GDPR: identity and contact details, purpose, recipients,
categories where data is not collected from the person, every State abroad with the guarantee
or exception, and "as a minimum" whatever is needed to exercise the FADP rights and guarantee
transparent processing. A Swiss-led notice therefore drops the GDPR legal-basis column (an
optional justification column replaces it), the Art. 13(2)(e) "do you have to give us your
data" section and the Art. 21 GDPR objection box, and states the FADP rights instead.

Complaints: the FDPIC handles reports and tells the reporter what it did and the result of any
investigation (Art. 49(1), (4)); civil claims under Art. 32. Withdrawing consent in a Swiss-led
notice is good practice (P13 with FADP sources: processing against express wishes needs a
justification, Art. 30(2)(b)); the notice says withdrawal takes effect from then on and does
not undo what was done before.

**Swiss points documented, not generated.** Art. 20 exemptions (person already informed,
processing required by law, legal confidentiality, Art. 27 media; for indirect collection,
impossible or disproportionate effort) and restrictions (20(3)); Art. 26 restrictions of access.

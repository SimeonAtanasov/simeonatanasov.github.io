## Part V. South Africa: Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)

Added 13 November 2026. Row IDs start with V. How each text was read, and what it says, is in
`build/za/notes.md`.

**Sources.** The Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 as published in Government Gazette 37067
(26 November 2013), read in the browser pane on gov.za through pdf.js text extraction: ss. 1, 3 to 7, 11 to 35,
55 to 59, 60 to 68, 69 to 72, 73 to 75, 77, 99, 110 and the Schedule (PAIA s. 51 as amended). No amending Act
found; the Information Regulator's own copy (uploaded August 2025) is the same Gazette print. The Regulations
relating to the Protection of Personal Information, 2018 (GN R1383) as amended with effect from 17 April 2025
(GN 6126): regs 2 (objections, Form 1; 2.4 the right to object notified at collection), 3 (correction and
deletion, Form 2, written notice within 30 days), 4, 6 (written consent to electronic direct marketing, Form 4;
6.4 opt-out is not consent), 7 (complaints, Form 5). The Regulations relating to the Processing of Data
Subjects' Health Information by Certain Responsible Parties, 2026 (GN 7198, Gazette 54268, 6 March 2026, in
force on publication; no notice content). The Regulator's guidance notes on Information Officers (2021), on
direct marketing (issued late 2024 or later) and on children (2021), its Rules of Procedure (October 2021), its
codes of conduct page (issued: credit bureaus, banking industry) and its contact page (Woodmead office, read
13 November 2026; the guidance notes still give JD House, Braamfontein). The shell's proxy refuses gov.za and
inforegulator.org.za; everything was read in the browser pane. Status checks: the POPIA and PAIA amendment
proposals of 31 August 2026 (no bill); the proposed codes (direct marketing, research, and others).

**Why this structure.** POPIA is built on eight conditions for lawful processing (s. 4). Processing needs one of
the six justifications in s. 11(1); people can object to processing on s. 11(1)(d) to (f) (s. 11(3)(a)) and to
direct marketing (s. 11(3)(b)). Special personal information (s. 26) and children's information (s. 34) are
prohibited unless s. 27 (with ss. 28 to 33) or s. 35 allows them. The notice duty is s. 18(1): the information
collected and its source, the responsible party's name and address, the purpose, whether supply is voluntary
or mandatory, the consequences of failure, any law authorising or requiring collection, an intended transfer
to a third country with its level of protection, and further information (recipients, the nature of the
information, access and rectification, the right to object, the right to complain to the Regulator with its
contact details). The 2025 regulations make the right to object a mandatory item (reg 2.4). The Regulator calls
its own document a "Privacy Notice", so a South Africa-led document is the "<name> Privacy Notice". POPIA also
protects existing juristic persons; the notice says so. It reuses the shared answers and adds per purpose: the
s. 11(1) basis (with the law, or the interest or duty), special personal information and how s. 27 allows it
(consent, a right in law, international public law, research, made public, the section for the kind of
information with its details, or a Regulator authorisation), whether a particular law requires or authorises
collection (named), whether supply is voluntary or mandatory, and the consequences of not providing it. The
location of each recipient, and a list of transfers outside South Africa under transfers (s. 72), give
s. 18(1)(g). The rest sits in a South Africa section of the form. With another law leading, South Africa adds a
section "Additional information under South Africa's POPIA", framed by the processing (s. 3(1)(b)). South
Africa sits after Australia and before California in the order of laws.

In a South Africa-led notice the WP260 delivery rows (D01, D03, D05 to D08, D14) stay as good practice and P21
(first layer) stays as good practice. The GDPR DPO, children, joint-controller, "is providing the data
required" questions and the Article 22 logic and effect are not shown when South Africa leads.

Not modelled: public bodies beyond a warning (the s. 11(1)(e) basis is accepted only for them), the exclusions
and exemptions (ss. 6, 7, 37, 38), the s. 18(4) exceptions to notification, directories (s. 70), information
matching programmes, credit reporting beyond the prior authorisation check, the PAIA manual itself, the
Consumer Protection Act, the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, the National Credit Act and other
sector laws, and languages other than English.

**Independent review, 13 November 2026.** 18 findings (3 high, 3 medium, 12 low), all applied after re-reading
ss. 11(2)(b), 18(1)(g), 23(1)(b), 23(3), 25, 27(1)(d), 28(1), (3), 33(1) and 77(1)(f) in the Gazette copy, the
Regulator's contact page and the Guidance Note on Information Officers (1.3, 2.2, 13.1). The location test no
longer lets a postcode override other places, keeps EMEA, Africa and unnamed backup or recovery sites as leftover
words, and counts ambiguous names and codes only right next to a South African name; the level of protection the
country affords is required for every transfer row except one resting on the country's own law, for the
organisation's own offices abroad, and for the countries where an organisation not domiciled in South Africa
processes; the access fee is the prescribed fee, not PAIA's, and only objections and correction are said to be free;
section 28 is described by how it applies; a private body's s. 33 ground is lawful collection only; complaints to the
Regulator are no longer "at any time", the unconfirmed postal address is gone, and a code's complaints procedure comes
first. The postcode, multi-country region and backup-site rules were applied to the Australia location test too (no
earlier output changed). Details in `build/review-2026-11-13-za/review-fixes.md`.

**Why this structure.** The APPs are not built on legal bases. An organisation collects personal
information that is reasonably necessary for its functions or activities, by lawful and fair means (APP 3),
and uses or discloses it for the purpose of collection, or for a secondary purpose with consent or under an
exception (APP 6). Sensitive information needs consent or an APP 3.4 exception (APP 3.3). The document
duties are two: an APP privacy policy with the APP 1.4 content (kinds of information, how collected and
held, purposes, access and correction, complaints, overseas disclosure with the countries) and, from
10 December 2026, the APP 1.8 information on automated decisions; and, at or before collection, the APP 5.2
matters (identity and contact details, collection from others, a law requiring collection, purposes, main
consequences, usual disclosures, overseas disclosure). The OAIC lets an APP 5 notice point to parts of the
policy (Chapter 5, 5.6), so an Australia-led document is the "<name> Privacy Policy" and carries the APP 5
matters too; a short collection notice at each collection point is a delivery row. It reuses the shared
answers and adds per purpose: whether it involves sensitive information and how APP 3.3 or 3.4 allows it,
whether an Australian law or court or tribunal order requires or authorises the collection (named), and the
main consequences of not providing the information. The location of each recipient, and a list of
disclosures to overseas recipients under transfers, give APP 1.4(f), (g), 5.2(i), (j) and APP 8. The rest sits
in an Australia section of the form. With another law leading, Australia adds a section "Additional
information under Australia's Privacy Act". Australia sits after Singapore and before California in the
order of laws.

In an Australia-led policy the WP260 delivery rows (D01, D03, D05 to D08, D14) stay as good practice and P21
(first layer) stays as good practice. The GDPR DPO, children, joint-controller, "is providing the data
required" questions and the Article 22 logic and effect are not shown when Australia leads.

Not modelled: agencies (APP 1 is the same, but access within 30 days without charge, the Information
Publication Scheme and the Australian Government Agencies Privacy Code differ), credit reporting (Part IIIA),
notifiable data breaches (Part IIIC), unsolicited personal information (APP 4), government related
identifiers (APP 9), quality (APP 10), the consumer data right, tax file numbers, the statutory tort
(Schedule 2), State and Territory health records and public sector laws, the Children's Online Privacy Code
beyond a check, and languages other than English.

**Independent review, 13 November 2026.** 20 findings (5 high, 8 medium, 7 low), all applied after re-reading
ss. 6C(1), 6D, 6E, 7B(2), 7B(3), 17, 18 and APP 12.9, 13.2, 13.3 in Compilation No. 104: a small business
operator not bound by the APPs gets a voluntary policy with no OAIC route; the location test no longer lets
any four-digit number make a place Australian; "own offices" holds only for the organisation itself; every
placeholder comes with a missing row (now a test); the recipients and overseas lists agree (storage-only
providers apart, unlisted overseas recipients missing, "no recipients" overridden by overseas rows); a blank
source, unanswered automated decisions and an agency's access charge are missing; permitted general
situations are conditions; employee records are said to be exempt; the response and complaint periods are
checked against 30 days; the correction and refusal sentences follow APP 12.9 and 13.2. Details in
`build/review-2026-11-13-au/review-fixes.md`.

**Location test, 13 November 2026 (from the South Africa review).** U10's location test (`auWhere`) no longer lets a
postcode after a state, a territory or "Australia" override other places ("Sydney NSW 2000 and Reykjavik" is
"unknown"; a street, a building, a level or a PO Box still goes), keeps APAC, Asia-Pacific and Oceania as leftover
words ("Sydney and APAC" is "unknown"), and keeps unnamed backup, disaster recovery, failover and replica sites as
leftover words unless an Australian place or region follows ("Sydney, backups in Melbourne" stays "au"). No earlier
scenario output changed; three regression scenarios cover it (`rv_au_pc_override`, `rv_au_apac`, `rv_au_dr`).

## Part Q. China: Personal Information Protection Law and the Network Data Security Management Regulations

Added 12 November 2026. Row IDs start with Z (C and Q were taken). How each text was read, and what it
says, is in `build/cn/notes.md`.

**Sources.** The Personal Information Protection Law of the People's Republic of China (PIPL), adopted
20 August 2021, in force 1 November 2021, not amended since: all 74 articles read in Chinese on the CAC's
official page. The Network Data Security Management Regulations (State Council Decree No. 790, in force
1 January 2025): Arts. 2, 21 to 28, 34 to 39 and 42 read in Chinese on cac.gov.cn. The Provisions on the
Cyber Protection of Children's Personal Information (CAC Order No. 4, 2019): Arts. 2, 8 to 10. The
Measures for Personal Information Protection Compliance Audits (in force 1 May 2025): Art. 12 (the
1 million threshold for the officer). The CAC announcement of 18 July 2025 on reporting personal
information protection officers. The Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (CAC
Order No. 16, 22 March 2024): Arts. 5, 7 and 8 as quoted (the reviewer read them through the CAC page). The
Chinese text is the only authentic text; the English in the notice is a translation.

**Why this structure.** The PIPL works like the GDPR: a ground for each purpose (Art. 13, with consent as
ground (1) and six grounds that need no consent), a notice before processing (Art. 17), and rights. Its
own points are what the notice must name for every recipient that is another processor (Art. 23) and
every overseas recipient (Art. 39), with separate consent where consent is the ground; the necessity and
impact of sensitive personal information (Art. 30), the personal information of minors under 14 included;
the choices for personalised push and marketing (Art. 24); dedicated rules for children under 14 (Art.
31); and, since 2025, the handling of data after the retention period and the deregistration of accounts
(Regulations Art. 21). A China-led notice reuses the shared answers and adds per purpose the Art. 13 ground,
whether the purpose uses sensitive personal information (with why), whether it uses under-14 data, and
optionally its own processing method; per recipient that is another processor its contact, processing
method, categories and whether providing it relies on consent; and a list of overseas recipients under
transfers. The rest sits in a China section of the form. With another law leading, China adds an
"Additional information for people in China" section. China sits after India and before California in the
order of laws.

In a China-led notice the WP260 delivery rows (D01, D03, D05 to D08, D12, D14) stay as good practice, P19
(joint processing) becomes Art. 20, and P21 (first layer) stays as good practice.

Not modelled: the Art. 18 exceptions to the notice; public disclosure (Art. 25) and image capture in
public places (Art. 26); State organs (Arts. 33 to 37, warned); the choice of transfer mechanism (asked as
a check, not decided), and whether direct collection by a processor outside China counts as provision
abroad (no CAC guidance found; the check says to take advice); critical information infrastructure
operators and the volume thresholds of Art. 40 beyond a check note; large platforms (Art. 58; Regulations
Arts. 44 to 46); written consent where other laws require it; the recommended standards GB/T 35273 and GB/T
45574-2025.

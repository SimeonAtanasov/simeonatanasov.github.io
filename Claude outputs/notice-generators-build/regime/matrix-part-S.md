## Part S. South Korea: Personal Information Protection Act

Added 13 November 2026. Row IDs start with S. How each text was read, and what it says, is in
`build/kr/notes.md`.

**Sources.** The Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA, 개인정보 보호법), the version in force on
11 September 2026 (Act No. 21445), read in Korean on the National Law Information Center
(law.go.kr, lsiSeq=283839). Its Enforcement Decree, the version in force on 10 November 2026 (Presidential
Decree No. 36804, lsiSeq=291731): Arts. 14-2, 15-2, 16, 17, 17-2, 18, 28, 29-7, 29-9, 29-10, 30, 31, 32,
32-3, 41, 44-2, 44-4, 47. The PIPC's privacy policy drafting guidelines (개인정보 처리방침 작성지침, April
2026, 185 pages, privacy.go.kr) and its press release of 24 April 2026. The PIPC's recognition of the
EU and EEA states as equivalent (16 September 2025, until 15 September 2028, excluding resident registration numbers and personal credit information) through a law firm update and news reports only. Amendments not yet in force
(9 March 2027: AI development, Arts. 28-12 to 28-15; 1 July 2027: mandatory certification, Art. 32-2(1)
proviso) are not modelled. Korean is the only authentic text; the English in the policy is a translation.

**Why this structure.** The PIPA has a closed list of bases for collection and use (Art. 15(1)), with
consent the first; data processed without consent must be listed apart from data processed with consent,
with its legal basis (Art. 22(3)). Sensitive information and unique identifiers need separate consent or a
law (Arts. 23, 24). Provision to third parties needs consent or one of the Art. 17(1)(2) cases; entrusted
processing is disclosed (Art. 26); and every transfer abroad, including entrustment and storage, needs a
basis in Art. 28-8(1) and the Art. 28-8(2) items in the policy. The privacy policy (Art. 30; Enforcement
Decree Art. 31) is a document of its own, titled "Privacy Policy", with a fixed list of items: the PIPC
guidelines number them 1 to 24. A Korea-led document is that policy. It reuses the shared answers and adds
per purpose the Art. 15(1) basis (with the law, or what follows a refusal of consent) and whether the
purpose uses sensitive information, unique identifiers or resident registration numbers (each with its
basis); per third-party recipient the Art. 17 or 18(2) basis, the items and its retention; per service
provider its sub-processors; and a list of transfers abroad under transfers. The rest sits in a
Korea section of the form. With another law leading, Korea adds an "Additional information for people in
Korea" section that is the Art. 30 policy for people in Korea. Korea sits after Japan and before
California in the order of laws.

In a Korea-led policy the WP260 delivery rows (D01, D03, D05 to D08, D14) stay as good practice and P21
(first layer) stays as good practice. The GDPR DPO, children, joint-controller, "is providing the data
required" questions and the Article 22 logic and effect are not shown when Korea leads: the chief privacy
officer, the children-under-14 question, what follows a refusal of consent and the Decree Art. 44-4 items
replace them. Consent withdrawal is asked whenever the PIPA relies on consent.

Not modelled: public institutions' registered files and Art. 18(2)(5) to (9) (warned, S26); fixed and mobile
video devices (Arts. 25, 25-2) and generative AI services (warned, S33); business transfers (Art. 27) beyond
the notice; breach notification (Art. 34); the 2027 amendments (S25); a Korean version of the policy (S24).

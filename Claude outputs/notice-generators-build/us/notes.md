# US state laws: reading notes (12 November 2026)

Each entry: official source read, notice section, rights, timing and appeal, opt-out and signals,
sensitive data, scope. Verbatim reading in the built-in browser; facts below are paraphrased.

## Virginia: Consumer Data Protection Act, Va. Code 59.1-575 to 59.1-585
Source: law.lis.virginia.gov, sections 59.1-576, 59.1-577, 59.1-578 (history to 2026 c. 820).
- Notice 59.1-578(C): (1) categories of personal data processed; (2) purpose for processing; (3) how to
  exercise rights under 59.1-577, including how to appeal; (4) categories of personal data shared with
  third parties, if any; (5) categories of third parties shared with, if any.
- 59.1-578(D): sells or targeted advertising: clearly and conspicuously disclose, and how to opt out.
- 59.1-578(E): describe in the notice one or more secure and reliable means to submit requests; no new
  account may be required (an existing account may be).
- Rights 59.1-577(A): confirm and access; correct; delete (provided by or obtained about); portable copy
  of data the consumer provided, where processing is automated; opt out of targeted advertising, sale,
  profiling in furtherance of decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. Parent of a known
  child may exercise.
- Timing 59.1-577(B): 45 days, extendable once by 45 with notice and reason in the first 45; decline:
  within 45 days with justification and appeal instructions; free up to twice a year; fee or decline
  for manifestly unfounded, excessive or repetitive.
- Appeal 59.1-577(C): conspicuously available, similar to the request process; answer in writing within
  60 days with reasons; if denied, an online mechanism or other method to contact the Attorney General.
- Sensitive data 59.1-578(A)(5): consent; known child under COPPA. (A)(6) no sale of precise geolocation
  data (2026 amendment). (F) known child: no targeted advertising, sale, significant profiling without
  parental consent under COPPA.
- Scope 59.1-576(A): 100,000 consumers a year, or 25,000 and over 50% of gross revenue from sale.
  Exempt: public bodies, GLBA financial institutions and data, HIPAA covered entities and business
  associates, nonprofits, higher education. No universal opt-out mechanism duty.

## Colorado: Colorado Privacy Act, C.R.S. 6-1-1301 to 6-1-1314; rules 4 CCR 904-3
Source: statute from the 2024 C.R.S. PDF as reproduced on colorado.public.law (the official C.R.S. is
now hosted only by LexisNexis; leg.colorado.gov PDFs no longer served); rules: coag.gov "FINAL-CLEAN
2023.03.15 Official CPA Rules" PDF via pdf.js; the 2025 amendment redline (coag.gov, Oct 2025) does not
touch Rules 6.02 to 6.04.
- Notice 6-1-1308(1)(a): (I) categories of personal data collected or processed by the controller or a
  processor; (II) purposes for which the categories are processed; (III) how and where to exercise rights,
  including the controller's contact information and how to appeal; (IV) categories shared with third
  parties; (V) categories of third parties. (1)(b) sale or targeted advertising: clearly and conspicuously
  disclose, and the manner to opt out.
- 6-1-1306(1)(a)(III): the opt-out method clearly and conspicuously in the privacy notice AND in a clear,
  conspicuous, readily accessible location outside it. (IV)(B): from 1 July 2024, honour a user-selected
  universal opt-out mechanism meeting the AG's technical specifications (targeted advertising, sale).
  (IV)(C) consent after the signal: notice of choices, categories, purposes, how to withdraw.
  (II): an authorised agent can opt out for the consumer, including through browser setting, extension,
  global device setting.
- Rule 6.02: meaningful understanding; no separate Colorado notice or section needed if it meets all
  requirements and makes clear Colorado consumers have the 6-1-1306 rights; concrete and definitive,
  clearly labelled; conspicuous "privacy" link on homepage or app store/download page, and in the app
  settings menu; offline version where interaction is offline; specific.
- Rule 6.03(A): (1) comprehensive online and offline description, linked so each category maps to its
  purpose: (a) categories processed, incl. whether children's data or other sensitive data, granular
  (examples: contact information, government ID numbers, payment information, information from cookies,
  data revealing religious affiliation, medical data); (b) purpose per category; (c) whether data provided
  for a purpose is sold or used for targeted advertising or significant profiling; (d) categories sold or
  shared; (e) categories of third parties, granular (analytics companies, data brokers, third-party
  advertisers, payment processors, lenders, other merchants, government agencies). (2) Profiling for
  significant decisions: Rule 9.03 disclosures. (3) list of rights. (4) methods: (a) instructions per
  method; (b) how an authorised agent opts out; (c) a clear and conspicuous opt-out method or links;
  (d) the authentication process (consumer and agent); (e) how universal opt-out requests are processed.
  (5) sensitive data inferences deleted within 24 hours: which, and the timeline. (6) contact information.
  (7) appeal instructions. (8) date last updated.
- Rule 6.04: notify material changes (categories, purposes, identity, sharing, third parties, request
  methods) through the usual channel; secondary use needs consent.
- Rule 9.03: profiling for decisions on financial or lending services, housing, insurance, education,
  criminal justice, employment, health care, essential goods or services: in the notice, the decisions,
  categories processed, plain-language logic, how profiling is used incl. human involvement, any
  accuracy/fairness/bias evaluation and outcome, benefits and consequences, how to opt out.
- Rights 6-1-1306(1): opt out (targeted advertising, sale, profiling for legal or similarly significant
  decisions); access; correction; deletion; portability (with access, at most twice a calendar year).
- Timing (2): 45 days + 45 with notice and reasons; decline within 45 with reasons and appeal
  instructions; free, but a second request in 12 months can be charged per 24-72-205(5)(a).
- Appeal (3): conspicuously available, as easy as the request process; answer within 45 days, extendable
  by 60 with notice and reasons; inform of the ability to contact the AG about the result.
- Sensitive data 6-1-1308(7): consent; known child: parent's consent.
- Scope 6-1-1304(1): business in Colorado or targeting residents, and 100,000 consumers a year, or
  revenue or a discount from sale and 25,000 consumers. Nonprofits are not exempted by 6-1-1304(2)
  (to confirm).

## Connecticut: Connecticut Data Privacy Act, C.G.S. 42-515 to 42-526
Source: cga.ct.gov 2026 Supplement (revised to 1 January 2026), chapter 743jj; P.A. 25-113 (2025
amendments, effective 1 July 2026) included.
- Scope 42-516: business in CT or targeting residents and 35,000 consumers in the preceding year
  (excluding payment-only data); or controls or processes sensitive data (same exclusion); or offers
  personal data for sale.
- Notice 42-520(b)(1): (A) categories processed; (B) purpose; (C) the means to submit requests, incl.
  how to exercise 42-518(a) rights and how to appeal; (D) categories sold to third parties; (E)
  categories of third parties sold to; (F) clear and conspicuous disclosure of processing for targeted
  advertising or sale to a third party for targeted advertising; (G) an active email address or other
  online contact mechanism; (H) a statement whether the controller collects, uses or sells personal data
  to train large language models; (I) month and year of the latest update.
- (b)(2): conspicuous "privacy" hyperlink on the homepage, app store or download page, and the app
  settings menu (or similar); without a website, through a medium used with consumers, including mail;
  in each language in which products or services are provided; accessible to people with disabilities.
  (b)(3) retroactive material change: notify, and an opportunity to withdraw consent. (b)(4) no
  state-specific notice needed if a general notice meets the subsection.
- (c)(1): means must include (A)(i) a clear and conspicuous link to a page to opt out of targeted
  advertising or sale (consumer or agent), (A)(ii) honour opt-out preference signals (consented,
  no default setting); (B) conflict with a loyalty program or privacy setting: comply, may notify.
  (c)(2) charging after a signal: present the financial incentive terms.
- (a)(1): sensitive data only if reasonably necessary and with consent (child: COPPA); (G) revoking
  consent as easy, stop within 15 days; (H) no sale of sensitive data without consent; (I) no targeted
  advertising or sale where the consumer is known to be 13 to 17.
- Rights 42-518(a): (1) confirm and access, incl. inferences and whether data are processed for
  profiling for significant decisions; (2) correct; (3) delete; (4) portable copy (automated);
  (5) opt out of targeted advertising, sale, profiling for automated significant decisions; (6) for
  such profiling, if feasible: question the result, be told the reason, review the data, for housing
  correct and have it reevaluated; (7) list of third parties sold to (or all third parties sold to).
  Agents for opt-outs (42-519); parent of a child; guardian or conservator.
- Timing (c): 45 + 45; decline within 45 with appeal instructions; free once per 12 months; opt-out
  requests need no authentication but can be denied as fraudulent with notice.
- Appeal (d): within 60 days in writing with reasons; if denied, AG complaint mechanism.
- (e): never disclose SSN, ID numbers, financial account, health insurance or medical ID, password,
  security Q&A, biometric data in an access response; say it was collected.

## Utah: Utah Consumer Privacy Act, Utah Code 13-61-101 to 13-61-404
Source: le.utah.gov xcode, 13-61-302 (eff. 31 Dec 2023), 13-61-201 (version eff. 1 July 2026, 2025 c.
468), 13-61-203, 13-61-102 (version eff. 1 January 2027).
- Notice 13-61-302(1)(a): (i) categories processed; (ii) purposes per category; (iii) how to exercise a
  right; (iv) categories shared with third parties; (v) categories of third parties. (1)(b) sale or
  targeted advertising: clearly and conspicuously disclose how to opt out.
- Sensitive data 302(3): clear notice and an opportunity to opt out first (not consent); known child:
  COPPA.
- Rights 13-61-201: confirm and access; delete data the consumer provided; portable copy of data the
  consumer provided (automated); correct (from 1 July 2026); opt out of targeted advertising and sale.
  No profiling opt-out. No appeal process in the statute.
- Timing 13-61-203: 45 days + 45 once with notice and reasons; decline with reasons within 45 days.
- Scope 13-61-102: business in Utah or targeting residents, annual revenue $25,000,000 or more, and
  100,000 consumers a year or over 50% of revenue from sale and 25,000 consumers; from 1 January 2027
  also motor vehicle manufacturers with a vehicle data collection system. No universal opt-out duty.

## Texas: Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, Bus. & Com. Code ch. 541 (eff. 1 July 2024)
Source: statutes.capitol.texas.gov (current through the 89th Legislature 2nd Called Session), BC 541.
- Scope 541.002: business in Texas or products consumed by residents; processes or sells personal data;
  not a small business as defined by the SBA (except 541.107: a small business may not sell sensitive
  data without prior consent). No numeric threshold.
- Notice 541.102(a): (1) categories processed, including any sensitive data; (2) purpose; (3) how to
  exercise rights, including the appeal process; (4) categories shared with third parties; (5)
  categories of third parties; (6) the request methods required by 541.055.
  (b) sale of sensitive data: the notice "NOTICE: We may sell your sensitive personal data." (c) sale of
  biometric data: "NOTICE: We may sell your biometric personal data." Both posted in the same location
  and manner as the privacy notice. The wording is fixed by statute.
- 541.103: sale or targeted advertising: clearly and conspicuously disclose, and how to opt out.
- 541.055: two or more secure and reliable methods; no new account; with a website, a web mechanism;
  exclusively online with a direct relationship: an email address is enough. (e) an authorised agent
  for targeted advertising and sale opt-outs, including by browser setting, extension or global device
  setting (universal opt-out), with listed exceptions.
- Rights 541.051(b): confirm and access; correct; delete; portable copy of data the consumer provided,
  if digital; opt out of targeted advertising, sale, significant profiling.
- Timing 541.052: 45 days + 45 once with notice and reason; decline with justification and appeal.
- Appeal 541.053: conspicuous, similar; written answer within 60 days with reasons; if denied, the AG's
  online complaint mechanism (541.152).
- Sensitive data 541.101(b)(4): consent; known child: COPPA.

## Oregon: Oregon Consumer Privacy Act, ORS 646A.570 to 646A.589 (eff. 1 July 2024; nonprofits 1 July 2025)
Source: oregonlegislature.gov ORS chapter 646A (2025 edition, notes 2025 c.163, c.251, c.417), read
through WebFetch (the browser pane was refused by the site). Reviewer: confirm wording.
- Notice 646A.578(4): (a) categories processed, including sensitive data; (b) purposes; (c) how to
  exercise rights, including how to appeal; (d) categories shared with third parties, including
  sensitive data; (e) categories of third parties, with enough detail to show the type of entity and,
  where possible, how it processes data; (f) an actively monitored email address or online contact
  method; (g) the controller's identity, including any registered business name and assumed name used
  in Oregon; (h) a clear description of targeted advertising or significant profiling and how to opt
  out; (i) the request methods of 646A.576(1). (1)(a) the express purposes are stated in the notice.
- (5): methods include a clear link to an opt-out web page (or another method), and an opt-out
  preference signal meeting five conditions (no default; consumer-friendly; residency determinable);
  universal opt-out from 1 January 2026.
- Prohibitions (2): sensitive data only with consent (child: COPPA); no targeted advertising,
  significant profiling or sale where the consumer is known or wilfully disregarded to be under 16
  (2025 c.251); no sale of precise geolocation data (within 1,750 feet) (2025 c.251).
- Rights 646A.574: confirm and the categories processed; at the controller's option, a list of the
  specific third parties (not natural persons) to which it disclosed the consumer's data or any
  personal data; a copy of all personal data (portable); correct; delete (provided, obtained, derived);
  opt out of targeted advertising, sale, significant profiling.
- Timing 646A.576: 45 + 45; decline within 45 with appeal instructions; one free request per 12 months;
  opt-outs need no authentication. Appeal: decide within 45 days in writing; if denied, how to contact
  the AG. Agents for opt-outs, incl. browser setting, extension, device setting.
- Scope 646A.572: 100,000 consumers (excl. payment-only), or 25,000 and 25% or more of revenue from
  sale; motor vehicle manufacturers (2025 c.163); nonprofits covered.

- Until 1 Jan 2027: scope 35,000 consumers (excl. payment-only), or 10,000 and over 20% of revenue from
  sale. From 1 Jan 2027: 10,000, or 5,000 and over 20%, plus (3) third parties acquiring data (cut).

  conspicuously disclose, and how to opt out. (e) describe one or more secure and reliable request

  signals from 1 January 2026 (five conditions).
- Rights 12D-104(a) now: confirm and access; correct; delete (provided or obtained); portable copy of
  data processed (automated); a list of the categories of third parties the consumer's data was
  disclosed to; opt out of targeted advertising, sale, profiling for solely automated significant

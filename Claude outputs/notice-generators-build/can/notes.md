# Canada: reading notes (12 November 2026)

Canada has no single private-sector privacy law. PIPEDA applies to commercial activity across Canada,
to federal works (banks, airlines, telecoms) and to personal information that crosses a provincial or
national border. Quebec, Alberta and British Columbia have their own private-sector laws, declared
substantially similar, which apply instead of PIPEDA to activity inside those provinces (exemption
orders under PIPEDA s. 26(2)(b); PIPEDA still applies to interprovincial and international flows, as
the OPC's cross-border guidelines note). An organisation can be subject to several at once: the notice
therefore lets the user tick each jurisdiction that applies.

## OPC, Guidelines for obtaining meaningful consent (May 2018, page modified 2025-08-11)
Jointly issued with the OIPCs of Alberta and British Columbia; reflects PIPEDA and the three
substantially similar laws. "Must do" checklist: make privacy information readily available in complete
form while emphasising four key elements: what personal information is collected, with which parties it
is shared, for what purposes, and risks of harm and other consequences (meaningful residual risk of
significant harm must be notified); information in manageable, accessible ways; a clear choice for any
collection, use or disclosure not necessary for the product or service (conditions of service must be
integral and explained); obtain consent for significant changes (new purposes, new third-party
disclosures) before they take effect; appropriate purposes only; allow withdrawal. Express consent
generally when the information is sensitive, the use is outside reasonable expectations, or it creates a
meaningful residual risk of significant harm. Children: the OPC takes the position that, in all but
exceptional circumstances, anyone under 13 cannot give meaningful consent (parent or guardian consents);
the OIPCs of Alberta and BC and the Quebec CAI set no age, but Quebec's Act now does (s. 4.1, 14). Purposes
in meaningful language, avoiding vague terms like "service improvement"; distinguish integral purposes
from others.

## OPC, Guidelines for processing personal data across borders (27 January 2009)
A transfer for processing is a use, not a disclosure; no further consent needed for the original
purpose. Organisations must be transparent: "advising customers that their personal information may be
sent to another jurisdiction for processing and that while the information is in another jurisdiction it
may be accessed by the courts, law enforcement and national security authorities." Ideally at
collection. PIPEDA applies to transborder transfers even for organisations otherwise under a provincial
law.

## Quebec: Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, CQLR c P-39.1
Source: LegisQuebec, English version, "current to 15 October 2026". The browser pane got HTTP 502 from
LegisQuebec all evening, and the page was read through WebFetch, whose extractor returns paraphrase with
short quotes only. The element lists below are from that extraction; the reviewer should read the
French or English text directly if the site answers.
- s. 1: applies to personal information collected, held, used or communicated in the course of carrying
  on an enterprise within the meaning of article 1525 of the Civil Code; not to journalistic, historical
  or genealogical material for the legitimate information of the public.
- s. 3.1: the person exercising the highest authority is the person in charge of the protection of
  personal information and may delegate in writing; the title and contact information of the person in
  charge must be published on the enterprise's website, or made available by other appropriate means.
- s. 3.2: governance policies and practices that provide a framework for keeping and destroying the
  information, define staff roles and responsibilities throughout its life cycle, and provide a process
  for dealing with complaints; proportionate; approved by the person in charge; detailed information
  about them published in clear and simple language on the website, or by other means.
- s. 4: purposes determined before collecting; s. 5: only what is necessary, by lawful means.
- s. 4.1: information about a minor under 14 is not collected from the minor without the consent of the
  person having parental authority or the tutor, unless collection is clearly for the minor's benefit.
- s. 6: collect from the person concerned, unless consent, law, or serious and legitimate reason.
- s. 7: on request, inform the person of the source of information collected from another enterprise.
- s. 8, first paragraph: when collecting and subsequently on request, inform the person of (1) the
  purposes; (2) the means by which the information is collected; (3) the rights of access and
  rectification; (4) the right to withdraw consent to communication or use. Second paragraph, if
  applicable: the name of the third person for whom the information is collected; the name of the third
  persons or categories of third persons to whom it is necessary to communicate it for the purposes;
  the possibility that it could be communicated outside Quebec. Third paragraph, on request: the
  personal information collected; the categories of persons who have access to it within the
  enterprise; how long it will be kept; the contact information of the person in charge. Fourth: clear
  and simple language, whatever the means of collection.
- s. 8.1: before using technology with functions that allow a person to be identified, located or
  profiled, inform the person of the use of such technology and of the means available to activate
  those functions (read with the CAI's position that the functions are off by default).
- s. 8.2: an enterprise that collects by technological means publishes on its website a confidentiality
  policy in clear and simple language and circulates it by appropriate means; same for amendments.
- s. 8.3: providing information after the s. 8 notice is consent to its use and communication for the
  stated purposes.
- s. 9.1: products or services offered to the public with privacy settings default to the highest
  level of confidentiality without intervention; cookie settings excluded.
- s. 12: without consent, use only for the purposes of collection, except (1) consistent purposes
  (direct and relevant connection; never commercial or philanthropic prospecting), (2) clearly for the
  person's benefit, (3) fraud prevention or security, (4) supplying a product or service the person
  requested, (5) study, research or statistics with de-identified information. Sensitive information
  (by its nature, for example medical, biometric or otherwise intimate, or its context, a high level of
  reasonable expectation of privacy) needs express consent. s. 13: communication to a third person needs
  consent or a provision of the Act; express for sensitive information.
- s. 12.1: a decision based exclusively on automated processing: inform the person not later than when
  the decision is communicated; on request, the personal information used, the reasons and principal
  factors and parameters, and the right to have the information corrected; the person may submit
  observations to a member of staff who can review the decision.
- s. 14: consent clear, free and informed, for specific purposes, requested for each purpose in clear
  and simple language, separately from other information if in writing; help on request; under 14 by
  the parent or tutor; 14 and over by the minor, parent or tutor; valid only for the time needed; consent
  not given under the Act is without effect.
- s. 17: before communicating outside Quebec, a privacy impact assessment (sensitivity, purposes,
  protection measures including contractual, legal framework of the destination, including its
  principles); communication only if adequate protection; written agreement. Same when a person outside
  Quebec is entrusted with collecting, using, communicating or keeping it. s. 18.3: mandates and service
  contracts in writing with protection measures.
- s. 22: an enterprise using personal information for commercial or philanthropic prospecting
  identifies itself and informs the person of the right to withdraw consent; on withdrawal it stops.
- s. 23: destroy or anonymise once the purposes are achieved.
- s. 27: access, written transcript of computerised information; computerised information collected
  from the applicant (not created or inferred) in a structured, commonly used technological format,
  also to a person or body authorised by law to collect it, unless serious practical difficulties;
  reasonable accommodation for persons with a disability.
- s. 28: rectification of inaccurate, incomplete or equivocal information, or where collection,
  communication or keeping is not authorised by law. s. 28.1: cessation of dissemination or
  de-indexation where dissemination contravenes the law or a court order, or causes serious injury to
  reputation or privacy clearly outweighing the public interest and freedom of expression, within what
  is necessary.
- s. 30: requests in writing to the person in charge, proof of identity; s. 32: reply within 30 days,
  silence is refusal; s. 33: access free of charge, a reasonable charge for transcription, reproduction
  or transmission, approximate amount told in advance; s. 34: reasons, provision relied on, remedies and
  time limit.
- s. 42 and 43: application to the Commission d'acces a l'information to examine a disagreement on
  access, rectification or s. 28.1, within 30 days of the refusal or of the time limit. s. 81: the
  Commission may investigate on its own initiative or on a complaint by any person; complaints may be
  anonymous.
- s. 90.1: monetary administrative penalties for, among others, not informing persons under ss. 7 and
  8; s. 90.12 maxima; s. 91 penal offences.

## Alberta: Personal Information Protection Act, SA 2003, c P-6.5
Source: King's Printer PDF, "Current as of September 1, 2025", text extracted in the browser pane by
inflating the PDF streams and decoding the glyph codes. The Government of Alberta consulted on updating
PIPA from February to May 2026; no amending bill found on 12 November 2026.
- s. 4: applies to every organisation and all personal information, except public bodies, personal or
  domestic, artistic or literary, journalistic purposes, business contact information (s. 4(3)). s. 56:
  not to non-profit organisations except for commercial activity.
- s. 5: responsible for information in its custody or control and for service providers; designate
  individuals responsible.
- s. 6(1): reasonable policies and practices; (2) if a service provider outside Canada collects, uses,
  discloses or stores personal information for the organisation, the policies include the countries
  where this occurs or may occur and the purposes for which the service provider is authorised; (3)
  written information on the policies available on request.
- s. 7(2): no consent beyond what is necessary as a condition of supply. s. 8: consent in writing or
  orally; deemed consent when information is voluntarily provided for a purpose and it is reasonable to
  do so; opt-out (8(3)): notice in a form the individual can reasonably be expected to understand, a
  reasonable opportunity to decline, no objection within a reasonable time, reasonable given
  sensitivity.
- s. 9: withdraw or vary consent on reasonable notice; the organisation informs of the likely
  consequences unless obvious.
- s. 13(1): before or at the time of collecting from the individual, notify in writing or orally (a) the
  purposes and (b) the name or position name or title of a person able to answer questions about the
  collection; (4) not for deemed consent under 8(2).
- s. 13.1: an organisation that uses a service provider outside Canada to collect information with
  consent, or transfers such information to one, must before or at the time of collecting or
  transferring notify in writing or orally (a) how the individual may obtain access to written
  information about its policies and practices on service providers outside Canada, and (b) the name or
  position name or title of a person able to answer questions about them. In addition to s. 13.
- s. 24: access to personal information and information about its use or disclosure (purposes and the
  persons to whom and circumstances in which disclosed); s. 25 correction; s. 26 written request;
  s. 28: respond within 45 days; s. 32: reasonable fee (none for personal employee information or
  correction), written estimate first.
- s. 46 and 47: review of a decision, act or failure to act, or a complaint, to the Commissioner, in
  writing; review requests within 30 days of being notified of the decision.

## British Columbia: Personal Information Protection Act, SBC 2003, c 63
Source: bclaws.gov.bc.ca, "current to November 10, 2026", browser pane. Changes not in force (review): S.B.C. 2025, c. 31 (Bill 28,
Business Practices and Consumer Protection Amendment Act (No. 2), 2025), ss. 16 to 26, amending PIPA for
credit reporting agencies (credit freezes and scores, new s. 24.1), in force 1 August 2027 (B.C. Reg.
126/2026). No amendment on extraterritorial
reach in the text; Clearview AI Inc. v. BC (IPC), 2026 BCCA 67 (18 February 2026) held PIPA applies to a
foreign organisation with a real and substantial connection to BC, such as collecting BC residents'
information online (secondary sources).
- s. 3: applies to every organisation, except personal or domestic, journalistic, artistic or literary
  purposes, where PIPEDA applies, where FOIPPA applies, and others.
- s. 4(3) to (5): designate individuals responsible; make available to the public the position name or
  title and contact information of each.
- s. 5: policies and practices, a complaints process, information about both available on request.
- s. 7(1): no consent unless the s. 10(1) information was provided; (2) no consent beyond what is
  necessary as a condition of supply; (3) consent obtained by false or misleading information or
  deceptive practices is not valid.
- s. 8(1): deemed consent where the purpose is obvious and the information is voluntarily provided;
  8(3): opt-out with notice, reasonable opportunity to decline, reasonable given sensitivity.
- s. 9: withdraw on reasonable notice, the organisation informs of the likely consequences; it must
  not prohibit withdrawal; not where withdrawal would frustrate a legal obligation.
- s. 10(1): on or before collecting from the individual, disclose verbally or in writing (a) the purposes
  and (b) on request, the position name or title and contact information of an officer or employee able
  to answer questions about the collection; (3) not for deemed consent under 8(1) or (2).
- s. 23: access to the personal information, the ways it has been and is being used, and the names of
  the individuals and organisations to whom it has been disclosed; s. 24 correction; s. 27 written
  request; s. 29 respond within 30 days; s. 32 minimal fee (none for employee information), written
  estimate first.
- s. 46 and 47: review or complaint to the Commissioner; review within 30 days of being notified.

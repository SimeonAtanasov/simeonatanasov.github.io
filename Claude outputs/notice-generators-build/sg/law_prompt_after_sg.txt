Background. The generator's privacy notice covers the EU GDPR, the UK GDPR with the Data Protection Act 2018, the Swiss FADP, Brazil's LGPD, Canada's PIPEDA with the Quebec, Alberta and British Columbia private-sector laws, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act with its 2025 Rules, China's Personal Information Protection Law with the Network Data Security Management Regulations, Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information, South Korea's Personal Information Protection Act, Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act 2012, the California CCPA and the consumer privacy laws of 22 other US states. Its rules are in the project doc claude/notice-generators-matrix.md (Parts B to T, open points in Part I). As of 13 November 2026 it assumes:
- California: the CCPA statute and the CCPA regulations, both effective 1 January 2026 (ADMT, risk assessment and cybersecurity audit package approved 22 September 2025). Revenue threshold $26,625,000 from 1 January 2025, adjusted in January of odd years (next: January 2027). The CPPA held preliminary rulemaking on notices and disclosures, employee data, friction in exercising rights and opt-out preference signals (comments closed April and May 2026); no proposed text yet. ADMT uses that began before 1 January 2027 must comply by that date.
- UK: UK GDPR as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025; the Information Commission replaced the Information Commissioner on 30 September 2026; complaints to the controller under DPA 2018 s.164A from 19 June 2026; transfers under Articles 45A and 46 with the IDTA or the Addendum to the EU SCCs, and the regulator said to keep using the current versions while it revises them in 2026.
- Switzerland: FADP SR 235.1 (version of 7 July 2025) and Data Protection Ordinance SR 235.11 (Art. 8 amended 1 December 2025); adequacy list in DPO Annex 1 (United States only for Swiss-US DPF certified organisations; Canada where PIPEDA or an equivalent provincial act applies).
- EU: the EDPB 2026 coordinated enforcement action on transparency (Articles 12 to 14 GDPR) had no published report yet.
- Brazil (matrix Part N): the LGPD, Law 13,709/2018, as consolidated on 25 February 2026 (Law 15,352/2026 made the ANPD the National Data Protection Agency); ANPD Resolutions 2/2022 (small processing agents), 18/2024 (encarregado), 19/2024 (International Data Transfer Regulation and standard contractual clauses) and 32/2026 (EU adequacy, covering the EU Member States, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway); Enunciado CD/ANPD 1/2023 (children). The ANPD's regulation on data subject rights (LGPD Articles 9, 18, 19, 20) was not adopted; the ECA Digital (Law 15,211/2025) is named but not modelled.
- Canada (matrix Part O): PIPEDA, S.C. 2000, c. 5, current to 19 October 2026 (its data mobility amendments, S.C. 2026, c. 3, ss. 389 to 397, not in force); Bill C-36 (Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, which would repeal Part 1 of PIPEDA) at second reading in the House of Commons; the Privacy Commissioner's Guidelines for obtaining meaningful consent (page modified 11 August 2025) and for processing personal data across borders (2009); Quebec's Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, CQLR c P-39.1, as amended by Law 25, current to 15 October 2026 (read only through extracts because LegisQuebec answered HTTP 502); Alberta's PIPA current as of 1 September 2025 (the Government consulted on updating it, February to May 2026; no bill found); British Columbia's PIPA current to 10 November 2026 (changes for credit reporting agencies from 1 August 2027, S.B.C. 2025, c. 31).
- India (matrix Part P): the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (No. 22 of 2023) and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025, with the corrigenda G.S.R. 892(E) of 10 December 2025), read on MeitY's Gazette copies. Commencement under G.S.R. 843(E) and Rule 1: Consent Managers from 13 November 2026; the notice duty (s. 5, Rule 3), the rights and Rules 3 and 5 to 16 from 13 May 2027. The Removal of Difficulties Order, 2026 (S.O. 5458(E), 5 October 2026) changed only the wording of ss. 9(1) and 10(2)(c)(ii) and was read only through law firm summaries. A January 2026 proposal to shorten the 18 months was never notified. No s. 16(1) transfer restriction and no s. 17(3) exemption was found notified. Rule 14(3) as printed is missing words; the generator reads it as a published response period of at most 90 days (as MeitY's press release of 14 November 2025 does). The Data Protection Board of India had no published complaint link (MeitY announced an online platform and a mobile app) and reportedly no members appointed.
- China (matrix Part Q): the Personal Information Protection Law (in force 1 November 2021, not amended), the Network Data Security Management Regulations (State Council Decree No. 790, in force 1 January 2025), the Provisions on the Cyber Protection of Children's Personal Information (CAC Order No. 4, 2019), the Measures for Personal Information Protection Compliance Audits (CAC Order No. 18, in force 1 May 2025; Art. 12: an officer at 1 million people) and the CAC announcement of 18 July 2025 on reporting officers (within 30 working days), all read in Chinese on cac.gov.cn. Transfers: the Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (CAC Order No. 16, 22 March 2024; Art. 5 exemptions, including fewer than 100,000 people's non-sensitive personal information cumulatively since 1 January for a processor that is not a critical information infrastructure operator). No CAC guidance was found on whether direct collection by a processor outside China under PIPL Art. 3(2) counts as provision abroad. The Cybersecurity Law amendment in force 1 January 2026 does not affect the notice.
- Japan (matrix Part R): the Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI, Act No. 57 of 2003) as in force on 2 November 2026, its Enforcement Order (Cabinet Order No. 507 of 2003) and Enforcement Rules (PPC Rules No. 3 of 2016, in force 14 June 2026), read in Japanese on e-Gov; PPC Notice No. 1 of 2019 (designated countries: the 30 EEA states and the United Kingdom, for recipients subject to the GDPR or the UK GDPR); the PPC general guidelines (read in part). The amending Act No. 56 of 2026 (promulgated 17 July 2026) takes effect on a date set by Cabinet Order within two years and is not modelled: rules for people under 16, a new biometric category (facial feature data; the term may be specified biometric personal information, not yet confirmed against the promulgated text), statistics and AI development, entrustment, breach notices; penalties partly from 17 January 2027 (secondary source only).
- South Korea (matrix Part S): the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA, Act No. 21445, version in force 11 September 2026) and its Enforcement Decree (Presidential Decree No. 36804, version in force 10 November 2026), read in Korean on law.go.kr; the PIPC privacy policy drafting guidelines (April 2026, 185 pages, on privacy.go.kr), which number the policy items 1 to 24. The PIPC recognised the EU and EEA states as equivalent under Art. 28-8(1)(5) on 16 September 2025, until 15 September 2028, excluding resident registration numbers and personal credit information (announcement No. 2025-68 itself not found; scope from a law firm update and a news report). Not modelled: the amendments in force 9 March 2027 (AI development, Arts. 28-12 to 28-15, with a new policy item under Art. 28-12(5)) and 1 July 2027 (mandatory certification, Art. 32-2(1) proviso); video devices (Arts. 25, 25-2); generative AI services; public institutions' own rules.
- Singapore (matrix Part T): the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (2020 Revised Edition, current version as at 13 November 2026; Part 6B, data portability, not in force) and the Personal Data Protection Regulations 2021 (S 63/2021; reg 1A as amended by S 580/2026 from 1 September 2026, letting the data protection officer's contact be published in the organisation's record on the PDPC website; reg 12 as amended by S 86/2026 from 2 March 2026, adding the Global CBPR and PRP systems), read on sso.agc.gov.sg; the Personal Data Protection (Do Not Call Registry) Regulations 2013 (regs 15 to 17: 21 days); the PDPC Advisory Guidelines on Key Concepts (revised 29 April 2026; para 21.7 still names BizFile+), on children's personal data (28 March 2024), on NRIC and other national identification numbers (2018, being updated) and on the Do Not Call provisions (2021). Private organisations must stop using NRIC numbers, full or partial, for authentication by 31 December 2026; the PDPC steps up enforcement from 1 January 2027 (PDPC announcement of 2 February 2026; MDDI statement of 3 February 2026). Not modelled: breach notification (Part 6A), data portability, the Do Not Call provisions beyond a delivery check.
- US states (matrix Part M): 22 states, 18 in force and Oklahoma and Louisiana (1 January 2027), Alabama (1 May 2027) and Vermont (1 January 2028) later; New Hampshire, Nebraska and Kentucky were read only through secondary sources.

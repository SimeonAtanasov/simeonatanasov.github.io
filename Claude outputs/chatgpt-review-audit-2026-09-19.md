# Audit of the two ChatGPT verification reviews, 19 September 2026

What was checked, finding by finding: the digest entry or page text as it stands in the repo, the site's own AI Act digest data (built from the amended Regulation), and the primary source online where the finding turns on a fact (EUR-Lex, EDPB, CNIL, ICO, KVKK, CPPA, Oregon DOJ, Supreme Court docket, the Bartz order, the Munich court press release, the Europrivacy opinion).

Verdicts: MERIT = the digest or page is wrong or materially incomplete and should change. PARTIAL = the point is right but small (wording, a qualifier, a date precision) or is a design opinion rather than an error. NONE = no change needed.

Totals. AI Act review: 30 findings, 20 merit, 9 partial, 1 none. Cookie and EDPB review: 41 findings plus 3 presentation points, 27 merit, 15 partial, 2 none. Both reviews are worth acting on. The AI Act one matters most: the Practical AI Act Advice page contradicts the site's own AI Act digest on several points of law, and the AI assessment tool has two logic gaps.

## Status: applied 19 September 2026 (night)

Every MERIT and PARTIAL row below was applied the same night and the results committed: practical-ai-act-advice.html (38 edits to the source, regenerated with navigation), pages/privacy-ai-assessment/assessment.js (Annex I question, Unresolved outcome, triage note; 22 questions, suite 338), edpb-digest.html and its PDF (28 entries and 192 templated one-liners, full-date ordering, date note), cookie-digest.html and its PDF (18 entries, cover counts), the study pack (145 pages, v2 name) and the verification report (40 pages, v2 name, with a second-pass section). The two NONE rows and F41 were left alone. Cross-checked on GDPRhub: Binding Decision 2/2022 (Instagram, EUR 405m, children's business accounts and public-by-default) and C-654/23 Inteligo Media (13 November 2025). The EDPB Hoda letter (F06) is reworded neutrally; the PDF could not be opened from the sandbox, so its content rests on the reviewer's description.

## Second versions of the reviews (received after the apply)

Both reviews were re-issued with an "independent second-pass audit" section that checks this audit against the primary sources and accepts its conclusions, including the 192-row template point. Diffed against the first versions: the AI Act review has no new findings; its two wording notes (the page said "can keep adapting", so it did not strictly make adaptiveness mandatory; "registered nationally" is true only of Annex III point 2) are consistent with what was applied. The cookie and EDPB review adds one row, EDPB #342 (the Joint Opinion 2/2026 entry in the EDPB digest) to F32; that entry was already corrected (EDPB id 33). Its per-question table for the AI tool (21 design suggestions) was then applied where cheap: the description question asks for intended purpose, affected persons and deployment context; the capability question notes that rules-based systems can still be AI systems; the lifecycle question gains a note on market placement dates, significant changes and real-world testing; the external-data note asks for licence, lawful access, provenance and the GDPR basis; the guardrails question asks about testing and points to Article 50. Not applied, by design: its "remaining verification limits" (open questions it could not resolve, not findings) and its suggestion to add a full harm and residual-risk assessment to the AI tool, which is what the DPIA and FRIA modules and templates are for.

## Part A. AI Act review (study pack Part 2 = practical-ai-act-advice.html, AI tool = assessment.js)

| # | Finding | Verdict | What is actually there and what to change |
|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Tool never tests the Article 6(1) product route | MERIT | assessment.js aiResult() derives the regulatory level from prohibitedUse and highRiskAnnexIII only. A safety component of an Annex I product gets "Minimal". Add a third screening question (Annex I product or safety component with third-party conformity assessment) that also sets High. |
| F02 | "Unsure" on Annex III returns "Limited"; "No" returns "Minimal" | MERIT | Confirmed in the code (lines 723-728). "Limited" is the Article 50 transparency tier, not a name for uncertainty. Return "Unresolved: legal review required" for Unsure on either gate, and stop the fall-through where prohibitedUse = Unsure and Annex III = No yields Minimal. |
| F03 | Open-source GPAI exemption stated too widely | MERIT | Page section 6 and self-test 12 say all four baseline duties fall away for open-source models. Article 53(2) lifts only (a) technical documentation and (b) downstream documentation; the copyright policy and the training-content summary remain. The site's own digest (art-53) says this correctly. Also section 1 mixes the Article 2(12) system exclusion with the model rule. |
| F04 | Product route omits the third-party conformity condition | MERIT | Section 3 lists the product laws only. Article 6(1)(b) requires the product to need third-party conformity assessment; the Omnibus added 6(1a) to (1c) (non-safety functions such as user assistance are not safety components). Digest art-6 has both. |
| F05 | Machinery reference outdated; Section A and B treated alike | MERIT | Section 3 cites Directive 2006/42/EC. The Omnibus deleted it from Annex I and added Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 in Section B; Section B products get only Article 6(1), 60a and 102 to 112 (digest annex-1, art-2). |
| F06 | Article 6(3) carve-out omits the profiling override and the four conditions | MERIT | Section 3 gives one example (narrow task). Add the four conditions and the rule that an Annex III system that profiles natural persons is always high-risk; add that exempt systems still register under Article 49(2). Self-test 8 and the review card follow. |
| F07 | Not every high-risk system goes into the public EU database | MERIT | Section 3 and self-test 14: "All systems classified as high-risk must be registered in the EU's public database". Article 49 covers Annex III systems (point 2 critical infrastructure registers nationally; law enforcement, migration and border entries sit in a non-public section); Annex I systems are not registered there. ChatGPT's own sentence "those systems are registered nationally" is wrong for everything but point 2. |
| F08 | Transparency summary too broad and incomplete | MERIT | Section 7 gives three bullets. Article 50 has four: provider notice on interaction (unless obvious); provider machine-readable marking of synthetic output; deployer notice for emotion recognition and biometric categorisation; deepfake and public-interest text disclosure with the editorial-responsibility exception. The "hiring tool that generates content must disclose" example is loose. |
| F09 | SME fines are "whichever is lower" | MERIT | Section 8 and self-test 17 say "whichever is higher" for everyone. Article 99(6) applies the lower amount to SMEs and start-ups; Omnibus 99(6a) extends that to small mid-caps for the 3% and 1% tiers (digest art-99). |
| F10 | Penalty table incomplete | MERIT | The 3% tier also covers authorised representatives, importers, distributors, notified bodies and Article 50 transparency duties; Article 101 gives the Commission 3% or EUR 15m fines for GPAI providers. Label the table as the three Article 99 ceilings and add the GPAI line. |
| F11 | Governance and penalties dated 2 August 2026 | MERIT | Section 8 timeline row "2 August 2026: transparency, penalties, governance, national authorities...". Article 113(b): Chapter VII governance and Chapter XII penalties applied from 2 August 2025, Article 101 from 2 August 2026 (digest art-113, art-101). The page even carries the 2 August 2025 authority deadline two rows above. Self-test 16 and the review card repeat the error. |
| F12 | "The whole high-risk regime starts 2 December 2027" | MERIT | Section 8 last paragraph. Annex I is 2 August 2028; Article 111 grandfathers systems already on the market unless significantly changed, with the 2 August 2030 public-authority backstop. Say "Annex III obligations" and add the transition rule. |
| F13 | FRIA scope lacks its limits; credit pricing example | PARTIAL | The three groups are right. Add that the FRIA applies only to Annex III (Article 6(2)) systems and excludes point 2 critical infrastructure. The bank example should say the system evaluates creditworthiness, not "prices credit". |
| F14 | FRIA content, update and notification omitted | MERIT | Section 5 mentions only the DPIA cross-reference. Add the six content items, the update duty and the Article 27(3) notification to the market surveillance authority. |
| F15 | Log retention lacks the six-month floor | MERIT | Section 5 says "appropriate period, aligned with GDPR". Articles 19 and 26(6): at least six months unless Union or national law says otherwise, to the extent the logs are under your control. |
| F16 | Deployer escalation omits suspension | MERIT | Section 5 "monitor and report". Article 26(5): inform provider and authority without undue delay and suspend use; serious incident goes to the provider first. |
| F17 | Provider checklist incomplete | PARTIAL | The twelve items are a fair teaching list but miss corrective action (Article 20), CE marking (48), post-market monitoring (72), serious-incident reporting (73) and the authorised representative for non-EU providers (22). Add a line saying the list is the Article 16 core and naming the rest. |
| F18 | "Four roles" not exhaustive; SaaS example trigger | PARTIAL | Add authorised representative and product manufacturer. The SaaS example is in scope first because it places a system on the EU market; the output-use limb is the second reason. |
| F19 | Provider reclassification overgeneralised | MERIT | Section 1, self-test 2 and the review card: "modifying or rebranding turns a deployer into a provider". Article 25(1) is limited to high-risk systems (name on a high-risk system, substantial modification that stays high-risk, or a change of purpose that makes it high-risk). Fine-tuning a GPAI model is the separate Recital 109 rule. |
| F20 | Exclusions omit conditions | PARTIAL | Add "real-world testing is not covered" to the pre-market R&D exclusion and "subject to adequate safeguards" to the third-country cooperation exclusion (Article 2(4), 2(8)). |
| F21 | AI definition loses inference and makes adaptiveness mandatory | MERIT | Section 1: "can keep adapting its behaviour after it's deployed". Article 3(1): "may exhibit adaptiveness" and "infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs". Rewrite the sentence. |
| F22 | Prohibition carve-outs and thresholds incomplete | MERIT | Key takeaway "two of the original eight have carve-outs" is wrong: predictive policing (objective facts) and biometric categorisation (labelling lawful datasets) also have exclusions. The RBI list misses missing persons and Annex II suspects; Article 5(3) allows a 24-hour urgency route. |
| F23 | "FLOPS" notation | PARTIAL | The page defines FLOPS as floating-point operations, so it is internally consistent, but the abbreviation normally means per second. Write "10^25 floating-point operations (cumulative)". Add the Article 52 two-week notification and the right to argue against classification. |
| F24 | Training layers presented as law | PARTIAL | Section 12 and self-test 20: "one generic module fails" and "layer four is mandatory". Article 4 requires supporting measures, no curriculum. Keep the four layers as a recommended design and soften the two absolute sentences. |
| F25 | Deployer liability language | PARTIAL | Section 5 "deviating from documented use is where a deployer's own liability starts" and section 11 "deployers inherit provider failures" are both too strong. Deployer duties are their own; a provider breach is not automatically the deployer's. |
| F26 | Business score is triage, not legal assurance | PARTIAL | Design opinion. The description matches the code (legal review and audit cadence add factors only). Label the business level as an internal triage score in the tool intro and the pack. |
| F27 | Thaler "settled at the top" | PARTIAL | Cert denied 2 March 2026 (docket 25-449 confirmed). A denial leaves the D.C. Circuit ruling standing; it is not a Supreme Court holding. Reword. |
| F28 | Bartz approval date | MERIT | The order is dated and filed 20 July 2026 (confirmed against the order). Page says 21 July. 482,460 works confirmed. |
| F29 | GEMA v Suno is not a lyrics case | PARTIAL | Munich press release (31 July 2026): six musical works, memorisation in the model, outputs infringing, judgment not final. Page says "repeated the result" (fine) but frames both as lyrics and says both are under appeal; say "musical works" and "not final". |
| F30 | Article 4a missing | MERIT | The page never mentions the new Article 4a (special-category data for bias detection under six conditions). The digest has it (art-4a). Add it to section 4 data governance and to the tool as a data question. |

## What ChatGPT got wrong or overstated

- AI F07: "those systems are registered nationally" is true only for Annex III point 2. The rest go in the EU database.
- AI F27 to F29 rely on facts it could not verify (appeals); the dates it did verify (Thaler, Bartz, Suno) check out.
- Cookie F24: "involving minors is too broad" is pedantic; the CPPA's own notice uses that phrase.
- Cookie F13 to F16 are one drafting convention, not 190 separate errors; three template lines and a word swap fix them.
- The coverage table (237 entries with a finding) counts every BCR and accreditation entry as a finding because of the template word "Approves". Excluding those, the substantive corrections in the two digests are about 30 entries out of 791.
